# Maximum or minimum of a sine or cosine model (2 marks)

Course: Year 12 Mathematics Advanced. Type id: `y12-trig-extreme-2`. Total: 2 marks.

## Question form

A model such as h(t) = 14 + 11 sin(pi t / 6) is given. The student finds its greatest or least value. This version is for questions worth two marks.

Generator families: `y12-trig-model`.

## Sample questions

1. The depth of water at a jetty, in metres, $t$ hours after midnight, is modelled by $h(t) = 22 + 16\sin\left(\dfrac{\pi t}{6}\right)$. What is the minimum value of $h$?
   Key: 6

2. The water level in a tidal creek, in metres, $t$ hours after the first high tide, is modelled by $h(t) = 9 + 4\cos\left(\dfrac{\pi t}{6}\right)$. What is the minimum value of $h$?
   Key: 5

3. The depth of water at a jetty, in metres, $t$ hours after midnight, is modelled by $h(t) = 14 + 11\sin\left(\dfrac{\pi t}{6}\right)$. What is the maximum value of $h$?
   Key: 25

## What the engine can and cannot verify for this type

The page is the marked page the student sees: each line of working is ticked, crossed or left unchecked by the checker. The answer checker is the code that marks a final answer. The server pays exactly the criteria below, from exactly that page.

It can verify:

- States the amplitude or the midline correctly.
- States the maximum or minimum value asked for, correctly (the final answer).

It cannot verify:

- It cannot check a long written reason ("the sine is at most 1, so...").
- It reads only the labels amplitude, midline, maximum and minimum, followed by a number.
- A model with a domain restriction on t is not read, because the greatest value on that stretch may not be the greatest value of the curve.
- It does not require both the amplitude and the midline: either one, correctly stated, earns the working mark.

Rule used in every award below: a line earns a criterion only if the marked page ticks it. A line the page crosses, and every line after it, earns nothing. A correct final answer is the full mark, as it always was. A wrong final answer can never reach full marks.
