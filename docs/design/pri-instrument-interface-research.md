# Make Pri a quiet instrument on paper

Pri Learning's best interface should look like a well-kept exam answer book and behave like a precise scientific instrument. The page is matte paper. The student's ink is the hero. A machine reads each line quietly in the margin, asks when it is unsure, and marks in examiner shorthand only after the student pauses or taps Check. The "futuristic" quality should come from behaviour, not decoration: ink that keeps up with the Pencil, a reading strip that shows what was understood, physics-honest motion, Pencil Pro squeeze and haptics, and calculator-style numeric readouts. It should not come from glass, glow, gradients or mascots. The evidence points one way. Decorative detail measurably harms learning (g = −0.33). Bare right/wrong feedback in computer-based environments is close to useless (ES 0.05), while elaborated feedback reaches 0.49. Apple's own Liquid Glass drew a legibility backlash that forced a toned-down option. The 2025–26 "AI-generated" look is a recognisable genre of purple gradients, aurora glow, emoji and cards inside cards. Pri already has the right skeleton, which the earlier Leibniz study confirmed: a greeting with a generate bar, a rail, a hint ladder, Type/Write/Photo modes and a next pill. It also already has the right identity in `client/src/theme.css`: paper, one teal, copper corrections, indigo dashed uncertainty. What remains is to make that identity rigorous and to carry it screen by screen: Home, the Write canvas with marking, Hints, the syllabus map, Review, Exam mode, the Teacher view and a phone layout that treats shared Android phones as the main device rather than a fallback. Every choice must hold to Pri's invariants. Recognition stays answer-blind. The deterministic engine owns every mark. No claim goes beyond the evidence. Children's data is handled under DPDP.

## Futurism that ages well lives in behaviour, not in glow

Start with what makes an interface look generic, because Pri's running build still shows those tells. The Fountain Institute's catalogue of "vibe-coded" UI lists seven signs. Neon palettes of competing saturated colours. Dark mode with decorative glow or aurora blooms. Emoji used as icons. **Purple-to-indigo gradients.** **Cards nested three or four levels deep.** Multicoloured accent bars on every block. Status dots that don't map to any real state ([Fountain Institute](https://www.thefountaininstitute.com/blog/signs-vibe-coded-ui)). The same source calls overused fonts such as Inter "statistical artifacts from the model's training data, the visual equivalent of autocomplete". It describes glow dark mode as "a genre rather than a design choice" ([Fountain Institute](https://www.thefountaininstitute.com/blog/signs-vibe-coded-ui)). The earlier Leibniz study found Pri's running build carrying several of these: violet-to-teal gradient buttons with glow, pill shapes, three competing home cards, and marketing copy. The gap is restraint, not structure.

Even Apple's version of futurism shows the limits of material effects. Liquid Glass is technically extraordinary. It bends light through real-time "dynamic lensing". It shows specular highlights that follow device motion. Its shadows adapt to stay readable over text ([Apple WWDC25](https://developer.apple.com/videos/play/wwdc2025/219/)). Yet critics said it "adds zero information while adding constant motion". Low-vision users struggled with text over text, and in iOS 26.1 Apple shipped a "Tinted" control to calm it down ([Gulf News](https://gulfnews.com/technology/companies/apple-yields-tinted-control-in-ios-261-beta-4-tones-down-liquid-glass-after-backlash-1.500315176); [Infinum](https://infinum.com/blog/apples-ios-26-liquid-glass-sleek-shiny-and-questionably-accessible/)). Apple's own rules are stricter than most imitations. **Glass belongs only on the floating control layer, never on content.** Never stack glass on glass. Tint only the primary action ([Apple: Adopting Liquid Glass](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/technologyoverviews/adopting-liquid-glass.md)). Nothing reached the same conclusion with type: in Nothing OS 4.0 its dot-matrix face moved back to places where it "can define character rather than compromise readability" ([nothing.community](https://nothing.community/d/61774-nothing-os-evolution)).

Learning science turns this from taste into a requirement. A meta-analysis of 58 studies found that seductive details (interesting but irrelevant content) reduce learning by **g = −0.33**, by as much as −0.70 in some placements, and hurt low-prior-knowledge learners most ([WSU Research Exchange](https://rex.libraries.wsu.edu/esploro/outputs/journalArticle/Keep-it-Coherent-A-Meta-Analysis-of/99900601155501842)). Particle effects, mascots, ambient animation or a glowing backdrop behind a quadratic are not neutral styling. They are an expected cost to the student. Dark text on a light background also beats light on dark for acuity and proofreading across ages ([NN/g](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/dark-mode/); [cogsci.nl](https://cogsci.nl/blog/is-bright-text-on-a-dark-background-a-good-idea)). That is the evidential reason the writing surface should default to paper, not to Leibniz's dark-only theme.

Where should futurism live, then? The crafted references agree on three ingredients. First, a **constrained, honest medium**: Nothing's dot-matrix, Teenage Engineering's bitmap faces and grid lines, Playdate's 1-bit dither ([shadcn.io Nothing analysis](https://www.shadcn.io/design/nothing); [Blake Crosley on TE](https://blakecrosley.com/de/guides/design/teenage-engineering); [Playdate](https://help.play.date/developer/designing-for-playdate/)). Second, **motion that obeys physics and tells you where things are**: interruptible, momentum-preserving, directionally consistent ([rauno.me](https://rauno.me/craft/interaction-design); [Family Values](https://benji.org/family-values)). Third, **every on-screen number is real**. Territory Studio keeps "fluff" to a minimum and refuses random numbers even in film UI ([Pushing Pixels](https://www.pushing-pixels.org/?p=9769)). For Pri, the constraint should come from its own domain, not from Nothing's. Squared exam paper, the ruled answer-book margin, the scientific-calculator LCD numeral and the examiner's tick are things every CBSE student recognises, and nobody else in edtech owns them. Copying Nothing-style dot-matrix now risks the next cliché, because those design systems already circulate as copyable files ([shadcn.io](https://www.shadcn.io/design/nothing)).

The futuristic moment students will remember is behavioural. They write a line, and a faint typeset echo of it appears in the gutter. When they pause, a copper underline settles under exactly the sign they flipped, with a note in the margin. Apple's Pencil Pro gives a native vocabulary for this. **Squeeze opens a contextual palette at the pen's hover point.** A built-in undo slider exists. **Haptics are located** at a point on the canvas, `alignment` for snapping and `pathComplete` for a completed shape ([WWDC24 10214](https://developer.apple.com/videos/play/wwdc2024/10214/)). That is a sci-fi interface in the Territory sense: it guides attention and shows real state.

## The margin is the interface: mark handwritten working like an examiner

The Write canvas is Pri's most important screen and its real differentiator. Two facts frame it. Pen input supports maths better than keyboards: students produced **56% more non-linguistic representations** (diagrams, symbols, numbers), which mediated **9–38% better problem solving**. Students still *say* they prefer keyboards, the "performance–preference paradox" ([Oviatt PDF](https://ksreussbuehl.lu.ch/-/media/KSReussbuehl/Dokumente/Dienstleistungen/Laptopklassen/Oviatt_Computer_Interfaces_in_Education.pdf?la=de-CH); [Microsoft/Oviatt](https://download.microsoft.com/documents/apac/en-au/For-Schools_MS-Oviatt-Paper_V2.pdf)). Leibniz, the closest product to Pri's ambition, is the one learning app confirmed to give **line-by-line feedback on handwritten responses**, with a "split-part canvas" and a "scribble pad" ([Leibniz App Store](https://apps.apple.com/au/app/leibniz-education/id6757947940)). Its students called the UI "very beautiful" and asked for a self-marking mode ([Bored of Studies](https://boredofstudies.org/threads/free-hsc-math-learning-platform-created-by-99-95-atar.414777/)).

### Ink must feel like paper before anything else

Perceived quality starts with latency. Stylus psychophysics put the inking-latency threshold near **50 ms**. Perception is anchored to the visible hand and pen, not to the gap between nib and ink ([Annett et al. GI 2014](https://webdocs.cs.ualberta.ca/%7Ewfb/publications/C-2014-GI-Latency.pdf)). On a web canvas, Pri's ink must render outside React's state cycle, using coalesced and predicted pointer events. A per-point React re-render would push latency past that threshold. Ink & Switch name "wrapper latency" as a core failure of digital ink ([Ink & Switch lab notes](https://www.inkandswitch.com/ink/notes/all/)). The canvas should be Pencil-only, with fingers reserved for scrolling and undo. That follows Inkbase's lesson: let the non-dominant hand change mode without a trip to the toolbar ([Inkbase](https://www.inkandswitch.com/inkbase/)). Scratch-out erases without switching tools, as in MyScript ([MyScript help](https://help.myscript.com/math/overview/handwriting-experience/)). Barrel roll stays on stroke rendering, never UI control, and squeeze is one discrete action, per Apple ([WWDC24 10214](https://developer.apple.com/videos/play/wwdc2024/10214/)). The tool set stays as curated as PencilKit allows. Two inks (working and rough), an eraser, a lasso, and nothing else.

### Show what the machine read, then ask instead of guessing

Every serious maths-ink tool exposes the recognised form before acting on it. OneNote shows it in a Math Assistant pane ([Microsoft Support](https://support.microsoft.com/en-us/office/solve-math-equations-with-math-assistant-in-onenote-1b37bb8d-ecd1-40d7-8d0f-5e6e46547441)). MyScript gives a live conversion preview with alternatives ([MyScript help](https://help.myscript.com/math/overview/handwriting-experience/)). Mathpix shows confidence and **refuses to guess at low confidence**, embedding the image instead of inventing LaTeX ([Softpedia changelog](https://mac.softpedia.com/progChangelog/Mathpix-snipping-tool-Changelog-142777.html); [arXiv 2406.17859](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2406.17859)). Ink & Switch found that continuous background recognition "introduces unreliability and user frustration". Explicit triggers combined with implicit recognition worked better ([Inkbase](https://www.inkandswitch.com/inkbase/)).

Pri should combine these into a **reading strip**: a faint typeset echo of each line, right-aligned in the line's gutter, appearing after a short idle pause. Each line takes one of three states. *Read* is plain typeset. *Ambiguous* gets an indigo dashed underline on the token, and a tap offers two or three alternatives (5/s, x/×, 1/7). *Unreadable* shows "?" and a "rewrite this bit" ask. **A line below the recognition threshold is never marked wrong.** It shows "I couldn't read this clearly". This one rule separates misreading from mathematical error. It stops the false "wrong" marks AGENTS.md lists among the critical categories, and it makes answer-blind recognition visible to the student as trust. The recogniser sees only ink, never the expected answer or the hint content. The strip must therefore never "auto-correct" toward the right answer, and it must never re-render student work in the student's own handwriting style the way Apple's Math Notes and Smart Script do ([Stuff.tv](https://stuff.tv/?p=936474); [MacTrast](https://www.mactrast.com/2024/07/apple-managers-talk-about-ipados-18-math-notes-and-smart-script-features-in-ipados-18/amp/)). In assessment, machine output must always look like machine output. Math Notes' solve-on-"=" behaviour, a delight in a calculator, must be off in practice and exam.

### Mark in the margin, in a grammar students already trust

Examiners use a compact, line-anchored grammar. A tick sits at the point of credit. Crosses are rare. Annotations such as BOD (benefit of doubt) appear. **"There is no direct relationship between ticks and marks."** ([OCR script annotations](https://ocr-live-prd95.cambridgeassessment.org.uk/administration/cambridge-nationals/post-results/access-to-scripts/understanding-script-annotations)) CBSE maths awards **step marks**, partial credit for correct formula and method even when the final answer is wrong ([PW](https://www.pw.live/school-prep/exams/does-cbse-give-step-marks-in-maths); [Careers360](https://school.careers360.com/boards/cbse/cbse-step-wise-marking-scheme-2026)). Pri's margin rail should speak this language. Each written line gets a tick, a copper mark or an indigo "?". The total reads in board idiom ("2 / 3 marks"). Follow-through credit is shown explicitly ("ft") so a student learns that later working still counts after an early slip. The engine decides every mark, so the rail renders deterministic output only. The pattern mirrors Gradescope, where AI suggests answer groups and a human confirms them ([Apporto](https://www.apporto.com/gradescopes-ai-assisted-grading-explained)): any model-generated explanation arrives *after* the mark, in a visibly different typographic voice, labelled as an explanation and never as a mark.

The feedback content matters more than its styling. Across 607 effect sizes, feedback helped on average (d = .41), yet **more than a third of interventions made performance worse**, especially when feedback drew attention to the self rather than the task ([Kluger & DeNisi](https://pluto.huji.ac.il/%7Emskluger/files/Download/Kluger%20&%20%20DeNisi%20abstract.doc)). In computer-based environments, **elaborated feedback reached ES 0.49 against 0.05 for bare right/wrong** ([ACU Research Bank](https://acuresearchbank.acu.edu.au/item/86y84/effects-of-feedback-in-a-computer-based-learning-environment-on-students-learning-outcomes-a-meta-analysis)). ASSISTments, which combined immediate correctness feedback with on-demand hints, raised Grade 7 standardised maths scores in an RCT of 2,850 students, with larger gains for lower performers ([SRI](https://www.sri.com/publication/education-learning-pubs/online-mathematics-homework-increases-student-achievement/)). So each copper mark carries a one-line, mathematically specific note in the outside margin, written in teacher voice ("−6 + 4 is −2, not +2"). There is no "Wrong!" and no "Genius!". The note sits next to the line it refers to, because split-attention is a Mayer coherence and contiguity failure ([Mayer, Multimedia Learning](https://doi.org/10.1017/9781316941355)). The engine's step taxonomy should map to distinct, non-colour-only states: correct step, valid alternative method, arithmetic slip, conceptual error, "correct answer but the board expects a method", and could-not-read.

Timing follows the same evidence and the Inkbase lesson. Show reading state live, but **hold judgement until a pause or Check**. Live red crosses mid-thought break flow and encourage self-focus. The verdict itself should feel instant from the bundled engine, well under 300 ms of the tap. Only the elaborated note may stream in afterwards. A "check my working at the end" mode suits stronger students, since Shute's review links delayed feedback to transfer in higher-ability learners ([Shute 2008](https://doi.org/10.3102/0034654307313795)). The evidence on timing is mixed, so leave it as a student choice rather than a default.

## Hints, progress and motivation must earn the trust of 12-to-16-year-olds

### A four-rung hint ladder with friction, not a reveal button

Worked examples reliably help maths learning (**g = 0.48** across 55 studies) ([Kent State OAKS](https://oaks.kent.edu/hcri/meta-analysis-worked-examples-effect-mathematics-performance)). The IES practice guide recommends interleaving worked examples with problem solving and **fading** them: give the early steps, then require the student to complete more ([IES/WestEd PDF](https://dww-library-files.wested.org/files/5198508.pdf)). The danger is help abuse. In one Geometry Cognitive Tutor study, students clicked through hints to the bottom-out answer on **36% of actions**, and unproductive help-seeking was associated with poorer learning ([LearnLab](https://learnlab.org/research/wiki/index.php/Help_abuse)). Matuschak adds a design critique: long static worked examples make "eyes glaze over", and steps should be revealed one at a time or the student asked to contribute ([Andy Matuschak](https://notes.andymatuschak.org/Math_Academy)). Khanmigo deliberately refuses to just give the answer ([kidsaitools](https://www.kidsaitools.com/en/articles/khanmigo-review-khan-academy-ai-tutor)).

Pri's ladder should have four rungs: orient, strategy, partial next step, worked step. They appear as dots ("Hint 2 of 4"). The next rung unlocks only after the student writes something or waits a short dwell. The bottom rung becomes a worked step followed by **a fresh similar item**, so the answer leak turns into a faded example. Hint use is recorded honestly in the student's own mastery model. Each rung opens in a Family-style **tray**: one piece of content, overlaying the current view rather than replacing it, and growing in height between rungs ([Family Values](https://benji.org/family-values)). The tray anchors beside the relevant line and on the side away from the writing hand. Self-explanation boxes stay optional, because Barbieri's meta-analysis found that adding self-explanation prompts *weakened* the worked-example effect in its dataset. That conflicts with earlier work, so treat it as unsettled ([Learning Scientists](https://www.learningscientists.org/blog/2024/1/25-1)).

### Progress as a syllabus map with reasons, not a black box

Khan's mastery ladder shows how to make progress legible. **Attempted, Familiar, Proficient and Mastered** are worth 0/50/80/100 points, and Mastered requires success on mixed assessment ([Khan Academy Help](https://support.khanacademy.org/hc/en-us/articles/115002552631)). Math Academy shows what goes wrong without visibility. Its queue is effective but a "black box": learners cannot tell "where they're coming from in the map of the course" ([Matuschak](https://notes.andymatuschak.org/Math_Academy)). Near the end of a course, reviews outnumbered lessons by about **2.7 to 1**, which learners called "review hell" ([Frank Hecker](https://frankhecker.com/2026/02/08/math-academy-update-5/)). Leibniz tracks progress at the level of individual syllabus outcomes and offers a predicted exam mark ([Leibniz App Store](https://apps.apple.com/au/app/leibniz-education/id6757947940)).

The evidence argues for mixing. **Interleaved maths practice produced d = 0.83 on a delayed test (61% vs 38%)** in an RCT of 787 Grade 7 students ([IES WWC](https://ies.ed.gov/ncee/wwc/Study/88770)). Mastery-learning programmes average about **0.5 SD** ([Kulik et al.](https://www.academia.edu/81783373/Effectiveness_of_Mastery_Learning_Programs_A_Meta_Analysis)). Pri's daily set should therefore be mixed by default. The interface should say plainly that mixed practice feels harder and sticks better, so a dip in session accuracy doesn't read as failure. Every recommended item carries a "why this question" line ("Review · last seen 6 days ago · Ch 4 discriminant"). The review share is capped and visible. Progress is a **map of NCERT chapters and outcomes**: Mini Metro's "beauty through elimination", with consistent shapes for each node type and peripheral information pushed to the edges ([GDC Vault](https://gdcvault.com/play/1024250/-Mini-Metro-When-Less)). Each node shows the four Khan levels as a segmented bar, not a percentage. No study shows that showing students a knowledge graph improves outcomes, so the map is navigation, not an efficacy claim. A predicted board-mark range should appear only after enough evidence exists, always with its uncertainty band, and never as a guarantee. The no-unsupported-claims invariant applies to the student's own forecast too.

### Motivation without loss aversion, leaderboards or profiling

Gamification has small effects, and only the cognitive one is robust. Sailer and Homner report cognitive g = .49 against motivational .36 and behavioural .25, and **the motivational and behavioural effects did not hold in the high-rigour subset** ([Educational Psychology Review](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s10648-019-09498-w)). Duolingo calls streaks its "single most effective retention lever" and ran more than 600 experiments on them ([Lenny's Newsletter](https://lennysnewsletter.com/p/behind-the-product-duolingo-streaks)). Its own data shows 7-day-streak users are **2.4× more likely** to return. It also states openly that the streak uses loss aversion ([Duolingo blog](https://blog.duolingo.com/how-duolingo-streak-builds-habit)). That is a retention mechanism, not a learning one. It also hits Indian teenagers unevenly. Among rural 14–16-year-olds who use smartphones, **only 27–38% own their own phone**, and girls own phones less often than boys ([PIB/ASER factsheet](https://pib.gov.in/FactsheetDetails.aspx?Id=149118)). A daily streak penalises the student who borrows a parent's phone.

The DPDP regime adds legal force. The DPDP Rules were notified on **13 November 2025**. Everyone under 18 is a child. Pri needs verifiable parental consent and must not **track, behaviourally monitor or profile children, or target advertising at them**. Child-consent obligations apply from roughly May 2027 ([SCC Online](https://www.scconline.com/blog/post/2025/12/26/digital-personal-data-protection-rules-2025-key-highlights/); [Tsaaro](https://tsaaro.com/blogs/safeguarding-minors-online-understanding-parental-consent-obligations-and-behavioural-monitoring-restrictions-under-the-dpdpa-and-dpdp-rules)). Whether an edtech company's mastery model qualifies for the educational exemption is a question for counsel, not designers ([Bar & Bench](https://www.barandbench.com/law-firms/view-point/childs-personal-data-and-privacy-analysing-the-draft-dpdp-rules-2025)). The design consequences are clear even so. There are no public leaderboards and no peer ranking by default; Shute also warns against peer comparison. There are no notifications timed from usage patterns, no third-party behavioural analytics or ad SDKs in student surfaces, and nothing sensitive on lock screens of shared phones. Consistency becomes a private weekly count ("4 of 7 days") with rest days built in. Effort is measured in honest focused minutes, the way Math Academy defines 1 XP as roughly a minute ([Math Academy](https://www.mathacademy.com/how-our-ai-works)). The real motivator for this audience is the board exam, so "Ch 4 marks earned: 3 of 6 → 5 of 6" is both honest and sticky. Growth-mindset slogans do not belong in the chrome: 94% of interventions in one review had confounds, and overall effects are small ([Macnamara & Burgoyne](https://hhs.purdue.edu/skill-learning-and-performance-lab/wp-content/uploads/sites/43/2024/08/2023-90931-004.pdf)). Indian context also explains what reads as "cheap" and what reads as serious. PhysicsWallah's complaints centre on login failures and crashes ([Trustpilot](https://nz.trustpilot.com/review/www.pw.live)), BYJU'S on sales tactics ([Gulf News](https://gulfnews.com/business/markets/can-indias-troubled-edutech-app-byjus-retain-its-dominance-with-uae-school-children-parents-1.96894386)), and Leibniz's on settings that don't persist and a glitching home screen ([Leibniz App Store](https://apps.apple.com/au/app/leibniz-education/id6757947940)). Reliability is part of the aesthetic. Pen, theme and canvas settings must persist.

## A design system cut from graph paper, typeset maths and one teal pencil

The identity in `client/src/theme.css` is already correct in principle: "a pristine mathematical notebook crossed with a scientific instrument", one teal accent and four states that never look alike. The system below makes it precise. Hex values are the current Pri tokens. OKLCH values are starting points for regeneration and still need APCA and colour-blind validation. Nobody has measured them yet.

### Type: one maths voice, one interface voice, one instrument voice

The Leibniz observation that matters most is that it uses **one typeface (Computer Modern) for prose and maths**, so equations never look pasted into sentences ([Leibniz, live CSS inspection](https://www.leibniz.com.au/)). Pri keeps that move. Question stems and all maths are set in the KaTeX/Latin Modern face, and `.katex` is tuned down from KaTeX's default **1.21× size** to roughly 1.05–1.12em so its x-height matches the surrounding text ([KaTeX font docs](https://katex.org/docs/font)). The native app can later move to MathML Core with an OFL OpenType MATH font. STIX Two is the serif nearest textbook convention ([Overleaf STIX2](https://www.overleaf.com/learn/latex/Articles/OpenType-based_math_typesetting%3A_An_introduction_to_the_STIX2_OpenType_fonts)). Chrome needs a face with more character than Inter, which the AI-look literature flags as a default. **Instrument Sans** (OFL) balances "precision with subtle notes of playfulness" ([RightFont](https://rightfontapp.com/family/instrument+sans)). Geist (OFL) is the more neutral alternative ([vercel/geist-font](https://github.com/vercel/geist-font)). The third voice is the instrument: **Geist Mono or Commit Mono with tabular, slashed-zero numerals** for marks, timers, question numbers and counts, so changing numbers never jitter ([Commit Mono](https://www.freshports.org/x11-fonts/commit-mono)). This is Nothing's three-face logic ([shadcn.io](https://www.shadcn.io/design/nothing)), translated into a calculator LCD rather than a departure board. A pixel face such as Departure Mono (OFL) can be allowed for one or two tiny instrument labels at most, and never for maths or prose ([Daring Fireball](https://daringfireball.net/linked/2024/09/03/departure-mono)). Hindi needs an open Devanagari face matched by `size-adjust`. Noto Sans Devanagari and Mukta are the usual candidates, but this session did not confirm their licences or metrics.

### Colour: paper, ink, one accent, four honest states, one mastery ramp

All tokens should be built in **OKLCH**, because HSL lightness is not perceptually uniform across hues ([Evil Martians](https://evilmartians.com/chronicles/oklch-in-css-why-quit-rgb-hsl)). Linear's model is the one to adopt: regenerate themes from three inputs (base, accent, contrast), which turns high-contrast mode into a by-product ([Linear](https://linear.app/now/how-we-redesigned-the-linear-ui)). Use APCA as the design target (Lc 75 body, Lc 90 small text, Lc 45 non-text) and WCAG 2 AA as the legal floor ([APCA reference](https://github.com/Myndex/apca-introduction)). Leibniz's muted `#666` text on near-black fails AA (about 3.4:1), and Pri must not copy it.

| Role | Light | Dark ("notebook at night") | Rule |
|---|---|---|---|
| Desk / page | `#f2f0ea` | ≈ `oklch(0.18 0.012 250)` | Never a gradient |
| Paper (canvas, question) | `#fdfcf9` ≈ `oklch(0.975 0.008 85)` | raised ΔL +0.02 | Opaque always; static grain ≤ 4% |
| Ink | `#1c1b18` | ≈ `oklch(0.93 0.01 85)` | Muted ink ≥ 4.5:1 |
| Pri teal (the only interaction colour) | `#0b6e69` | ≈ `oklch(0.72 0.11 190)` | One primary action per screen; P3 chroma boost under `color-gamut: p3` |
| Correction (copper) | `#a04f24` | `#e0915f` | Underline + margin note; zero bounce |
| Uncertain (indigo) | `#4a54a3` | `#a3acec` | Always dashed + "?" glyph |
| Correct (green) | `#2d6a43` | `#7cc79a` | Restrained tick; never a celebration |
| Failure (red) | `#b3261e` | `#f2877d` | Technical failure / destructive only, never maths errors |
| Mastery ramp | teal hue 190, L 0.95→0.45 in 4 equal steps | mirrored | Segmented shape carries meaning too |

Pri should **not adopt Leibniz's earth-tone mastery scale** (`#c46a5a → #d4915a → #c9a86c → #a8c686`), even though it is well designed. It runs from red toward green. That relies on hue, it collides with Pri's copper correction colour, and it breaks WCAG 1.4.1's rule against colour-only meaning ([W3C WCAG 2.2](https://www.w3.org/TR/WCAG22/)). Leibniz's real lesson is to keep **mastery, marking and graph palettes in separate vocabularies**. A single-hue lightness ramp combined with a segment count does that and survives colour blindness. Teal against copper (blue-green against orange) should hold up better than red/green for deutan and protan viewers, though this report has not run a simulator to confirm it.

### Material and shape: matte paper content, glass only on the floating palette

The content layer (question, canvas, margin rail) stays opaque paper, flat, with hairline rules and a squared grid aligned to the 4 pt grid. The only translucent object is the **floating pen palette and the top status bar**. On native iPadOS 26 that is Liquid Glass. On the web it is `backdrop-filter` blur over a roughly 70% paper tint, with an opaque fallback under `prefers-reduced-transparency` and `prefers-contrast: more`. That fallback copies Apple's own accessibility behaviour ([Apple WWDC25](https://developer.apple.com/videos/play/wwdc2025/219/)). Elevation has three levels at most: flat paper, a hairline with ΔL +0.01, and floating glass with one soft shadow. Radii are 2–4 px on web content. The current `--radius-pill: 999px` token should be retired from interactive controls; Nothing also avoids pill buttons ([shadcn.io](https://www.shadcn.io/design/nothing)). Native glass controls follow Apple's concentric shapes. Secondary buttons can borrow Leibniz's observed "keycap" stacked bottom shadow (1–3 px) for physical tactility without gradients.

### Motion, haptics and sound: a delight budget that falls as frequency rises

Emil Kowalski's rules set the frame. UI motion should be **under 300 ms**, ease-out or spring, transform and opacity only, interruptible, honouring `prefers-reduced-motion`, and **never applied to actions repeated hundreds of times a day** ([emilkowal.ski](https://emilkowal.ski/ui/great-animations)). Family's principle completes it: the less often something happens, the more delight it may carry ([Family Values](https://benji.org/family-values)). Apple's default spring is critically damped (response 0.55, damping 1.0), and a 0.15 bounce counts as "small" ([Apple docs](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/swiftui/animation/default.md); [WWDC Notes](https://wwdcnotes.com/notes/wwdc23/10158)).

| Token | Spec | Used for |
|---|---|---|
| `ink` | 0 ms added; no animation ever | Strokes, erasing, reading-strip updates |
| `motion.ui` | spring, ~0.35 s visual duration, bounce 0 | Trays, sheets, palette, layout |
| `motion.mark` | tick stroke-draw 220–280 ms ease-out; chip scale 0.96→1, bounce 0.15 | Correct step reveal |
| `motion.correct` | copper underline draw ~200 ms, **no bounce** | Errors should not feel playful |
| `motion.hint` | tray height spring ~0.4 s, bounce 0.1; text fades 120 ms after settle | Hint rung unlock |
| `motion.next` | directional slide in the direction of progress + shared-element glide of question number; button text morphs "Check" → "Next" | Between questions |
| `motion.reduced` | ≤100 ms opacity only; state changes still visible via glyph + colour | Reduced motion |

Celebration is reserved for rare events, such as finishing a chapter's outcomes. It happens between items, never on the working surface, because during practice it is a seductive detail. Haptics are **Pencil Pro only and semantic**. `alignment` fires when a stroke snaps to the graph grid or into a part box. `pathComplete` fires when a written line is committed to the reading strip ([Apple UICanvasFeedbackGenerator](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/uikit/uicanvasfeedbackgenerator.md)). No haptic ever signals right or wrong while the student writes, because that would leak live judgement into the act of writing. Sound is off by default for classroom use. If offered at all, it is a short, non-melodic click for Check and step-commit, with nothing on errors. No cited evidence supports learning benefits from sound or haptics, so both stay optional craft.

### Layout and icons: an instrument panel that reflows to any window

iPadOS windows now resize continuously, so layouts must use container queries, not fixed portrait and landscape presets ([Apple: Adopting Liquid Glass](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/technologyoverviews/adopting-liquid-glass.md)). The base is a 4 pt grid with 8 pt rhythm. Line height is 1.45 for prose and 1.6 for lines with inline maths. The stem measure is about 68ch. Targets are at least 44 pt, above WCAG 2.2's 24 px minimum. Icons are SF Symbols on native for system actions. On the web, one open icon set is used at a single stroke weight matched to the UI font. A small bespoke set of mathematical glyphs, drawn on a 24 px grid, covers Pri-only concepts: √ check, ≈ uncertain reading, ∴ worked solution, Δ what changed. They extend the existing `client/src/components/Icon.jsx`. Every icon has a text label on touch. No emoji, no ✨ for AI.

## Screen-by-screen plan, in priority order

The priority order follows leverage and risk. The canvas is the differentiator and carries the highest-risk invariants. The token purge is the cheapest way to remove the AI look everywhere. The phone layout matters because about **90% of rural 14–16-year-olds have a smartphone at home** ([PIB/ASER](https://pib.gov.in/FactsheetDetails.aspx?Id=149118)), while no reliable data on Indian iPad ownership was found. Risk classes follow AGENTS.md. Any change to the handwriting or marking surface gets R4 review even when it is presentation-only.

| # | Screen / work | Core decision | Risk |
|---|---|---|---|
| P0 | **Write canvas with marking** | Reading strip + three recognition states; margin rail in examiner grammar; marks only from the engine; hold judgement for pause or Check | R4 |
| P0 | **Token and type purge** | Remove gradients, glow and pills; OKLCH regeneration; three-voice type; CI check fails on `linear-gradient` and on radius > 4 px in components | R2 |
| P1 | **Hints tray** | Four rungs with dwell or write-to-unlock; bottom rung → worked step + similar item; honest mastery credit | R3 |
| P1 | **Phone layout** | Single column; maths keypad + small handwriting pad + photo of paper; offline-first; profile switching | R2/R3 |
| P1 | **Home** | One heading, one generate bar, one facts line, one "why" line | R2 |
| P2 | **Progress / syllabus map** | NCERT chapter → outcome map; four-level segmented bars; reasons on every recommendation | R3 |
| P2 | **Review** | Your own past ink with its margin marks, then a fresh similar item; capped review ratio | R3 |
| P2 | **Exam mode** | CBSE answer-book layout; no hints, no live marking; examiner-annotated script after submission | R3/R4 |
| P3 | **Teacher view** | Pacing ribbon + anonymised ink snapshots; engine marks, teacher confirms overrides | R4 (auth/data) |

### Write canvas (P0)

In portrait, the most paper-like orientation for line-by-line working, a slim top bar holds the provenance chip ("NCERT Ex 4.3 · Q2"), the question number in tabular mono and a single teal Check. The question stem sits typeset above a ruled answer area with a copper-tinted left margin rule. The right margin rail is reserved for marks. Case-based items pin the shared stem and segment parts (a), (b), (c) into Leibniz-style split-part zones. A **rough-work column is visibly separate and never marked**, as in a board answer book. In landscape, the question moves to a left column, the canvas fills the right, and an inspector holds hints. The floating glass pen palette docks on the side opposite the writing hand, and squeeze summons it at the pen tip. After Check, ticks draw in, copper underlines settle under the exact faulty token, notes appear in the outside margin, and indigo dashed boxes ask "Did you mean 5 or s?". The total reads "2 / 3 marks · ft". An optional explanation streams in afterwards in a smaller, differently set voice, labelled "Explanation", and it can never change the mark. After a wrong answer the pill offers "Try a similar one". Type and Photo remain alternative inputs that go through the same reading-then-marking pipeline.

### Home (P1)

Home is one sentence and one action. A greeting in plain ink (no gradient text, and the split "P | ri" logo fixed). The generate bar with chapter and outcome filters. One facts line in instrument numerals: "Today 4 / 10 · 4 of 7 days this week · Next review: Rational numbers". One recommended set with its reason. GoalCard, DiamondTrack and the suggestion card collapse into that line. There are no banners, offers or countdowns, which in Indian edtech read as cheap and untrustworthy.

### Hints (P1)

Hints open as a tray anchored to the current line, opposite the writing hand. Rung dots show cost before the student taps. Rung 1 asks an orienting question. Rung 2 names a strategy. Rung 3 gives the next step with the result blanked for the student to write. Rung 4 shows the worked step, morphing the expression from line n to line n+1 with the changed term highlighted, as in Photomath's animated steps ([Photomath](https://apps.apple.com/ZA/app/id919087726)). It then queues a similar item. Hint content comes from the curriculum and the engine, never from the recogniser, and the recogniser never sees it.

### Progress and syllabus map (P2)

A Mini Metro-like map of the NCERT chapters for the student's class, with prerequisite lines between them. Each node opens into outcomes with four-segment Khan-style bars and explicit evidence ("3 unaided correct across 2 days"). Review-due states are neutral, not alarming. A side annotation names the weakest prerequisite link and offers one action. A predicted marks range appears per chapter only once there is enough evidence, always with its band visible.

### Review (P2)

Review is a "mistakes notebook". The student sees their own past ink with Pri's margin marks exactly as given, then solves a **fresh** similar item rather than re-solving the same one. Sets are interleaved by default, the header says why mixed practice feels harder, and the ratio of review to new work stays visibly capped to avoid "review hell".

### Exam mode (P2)

Exam mode is a separate, clearly signalled room, not a variant of practice. It follows the CBSE section structure (reported as 50% competency-focused items, 20% MCQ and 30% constructed response for 2025–26; [Oswaal](https://oswaalbooks.com/products/cbse-15-sample-question-papers-class-10-mathematics-basic-for-2026-board-exam-as-per-cbse-sample-question-paper-issued-on-30-july-2025-recommended-by-2025-cbse-toppers-with-chapter-wise-highly-probable-questions)). Those percentages come from publishers and should be checked against the official CBSE sample paper. There are no hints, no live reading-to-marking feedback and no Math-Notes-style solving. The timer is quiet tabular mono and can be hidden. Timed pressure is a plausible anxiety trigger, although Boaler's claim is advocacy-grade ([Stanford GSE](https://ed.stanford.edu/news/learning-math-without-fear)), so timing is opt-in. On submit, the student receives the full script annotated in examiner grammar, which uses delayed feedback deliberately. Existing timed or competitive pages (`Rush.jsx`, `Match.jsx`) should be audited against these rules: private by default for minors, never inside practice.

### Teacher view (P3)

The teacher view borrows Desmos Classroom's **thumbnail pacing ribbon** and Snapshot: select and sequence anonymised student ink to project for discussion ([Desmos blog](https://blog.desmos.com/articles/introducing-the-new-desmos-activity-dashboard/); [Amplify help](https://my.amplify.com/help/en/articles/6763606-snapshots-select-and-sequence-student-work)). Gradescope's pattern clusters similar handwritten errors for a human to confirm ([Turnitin Gradescope](https://www.turnitin.ca/products/gradescope/)). In Pri, the engine's marks stand, and any teacher override is explicit and logged. The teacher sees only their own class, enforced by row-level security plus server authorisation. Projected ink is anonymised by default. Any classroom use of child data runs through the DPDP consent and exemption questions that counsel must answer.

### Phone layout (P1)

On a shared, mid-range Android phone the canvas becomes one column. The stem collapses to a pinned header. Input becomes a structured maths keypad plus a small handwriting pad that commits one line at a time, with the same reading strip and three states. "Photo of my paper working" (Leibniz supports photo and PDF upload) lets students who write in notebooks still get line-level marks on their own paper. Marks appear as a compact per-line list under the photo, keeping the margin grammar. The phone layout needs small payloads, offline practice with the bundled engine, profile switching for shared devices, and no notification content on the lock screen. On phones the margin rail becomes a right-edge column of glyphs, and hint and mark trays become bottom sheets, one primary action each.

### What is still unproven

Several things here are design hypotheses, not findings. No study in these notes tested what Indian 12–16-year-olds find patronising or premium. No reliable figure exists for iPad ownership in this audience. The OKLCH and APCA values are uncomputed. The idle threshold for the reading strip is a heuristic. No controlled study compares live and deferred step feedback for handwritten maths on tablets. Several Leibniz, Duolingo and Brilliant visual specifics could not be verified from primary sources. The first round of work should therefore ship behind evidence gates: a small usability study with real Class 8–10 students on real iPads and shared phones, kept explicitly separate from simulator evidence, before any claim that the design improves learning.

## Conclusion

The research changes what "futuristic" should mean for Pri. It does not mean an interface that looks like 2030. It means a machine that reads paper honestly. The most advanced-feeling thing Pri can do is make its own epistemics visible. The student sees what was read, what Pri is unsure of, which mark the deterministic engine gave and why, and where an explanation (and any AI) begins and ends. Those are the same lines AGENTS.md draws for safety, so the trust boundaries and the aesthetic turn out to be one design, not a compromise between two. The examiner's margin, an interface every CBSE student already reads fluently, carries that information more efficiently than any glass panel.

The second implication is strategic. In 2026 the cheapest way to look generic is to add effects, and the hardest thing for competitors to copy is domain specificity: NCERT provenance on every item, board step-mark idiom, ink that keeps up with the Pencil, uncertainty that never becomes a false "wrong", and a phone path built for borrowed devices. Pri's existing `theme.css` already names this identity. What remains is to enforce it in CI, prove the canvas with real students, and resist adding anything to the working surface that can't justify itself against a measured −0.33 cost to learning.
