## What was tested, and what was not

- **Data.** NESA's own "Mapping Grid" at the end of each marking guideline: every question part, its marks and the 2017 syllabus topic code(s) NESA mapped it to. Mathematics Advanced 2020, 2021, 2022, 2023, 2024, 2025; Mathematics Extension 1 2020, 2021, 2022, 2023, 2024, 2025, 2026. Every paper's parts add to its total (100 / 70). Metadata only — no NESA text is stored.
- **Not tagged:**
  - Mathematics Advanced 2026: NESA had not published the 2026 Mathematics Advanced marking guidelines (and so its Mapping Grid) on 18 November 2026. The paper is not tagged from Pri's own reading.
  - Mathematics Advanced 2019: Before 2020 the course was Mathematics (2-unit) on an earlier syllabus with different topics; it is not mapped.
  - Mathematics Extension 1 2019: Before 2020 Extension 1 was examined on an earlier syllabus with different topics; it is not mapped.
- **Unit of evaluation.** Marks share per 2017 topic (14 Advanced topics, 12 Extension 1 topics). A part mapped to n topics gives each 1/n of its marks.
- **Protocol.** For each test year Y ≥ 2021, fit on years < Y only, score against Y. Pre-registered before any number was seen: decay 0.7, top-k with k = 5 (Advanced) and 4 (Extension 1), primary metric mean total variation distance (TVD). Nothing was tuned after.
- **Metrics.** Top-k hit: how many of the year's k biggest topics the method also put in its top k. Spearman ρ: rank correlation of marks share across all topics. TVD: half the summed absolute difference in marks share — the share of the paper's marks the method put in the wrong topics.
- **Baselines.** (1) Last year's paper as it was. (2) Every syllabus topic equal (the 2017 indicative hours are not stored, so equal topics stands in for syllabus weight).
- **Small sample.** 5 Advanced and 6 Extension 1 test years. No significance test is claimed; the differences are descriptive.

## Transportability limit — read this before the numbers

Every test year was set on the **2017** syllabuses. The 2027 HSC is the **first** examined on the 2024 syllabuses (NESA: "2027 — First HSC examination for the new syllabus"). These numbers say how well each method would have ranked topics on the old syllabus, when the syllabus did not change between fit and test. They are **not** an accuracy for 2027, and no accuracy for 2027 is claimed. For 2027 the history is carried to the new outcomes through Pri's old → new map (`hscOutcomes.js`, part of it Pri's estimate), and new content no past paper could test is forecast from NESA's sample examination and syllabus weight alone, which cannot be backtested at all. A new syllabus's first paper may also differ in emphasis on purpose.
