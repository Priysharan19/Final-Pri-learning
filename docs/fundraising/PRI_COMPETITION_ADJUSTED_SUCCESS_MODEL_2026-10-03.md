# Pri Learning — Competition-Adjusted Funding & Support Success Model

**Research snapshot:** 3 October 2026  
**Companion to:** `PRI_FUNDING_SUPPORT_DOSSIER_2026-10-03.md`  
**Purpose:** Estimate Pri Learning's probability of winning specific funding/support outcomes after adjusting for observed competition, programme fit, Pri's current strengths, and missing external validation.

> ## Read this before using the percentages
>
> These are **decision-model estimates, not actuarial probabilities**. Where a programme publishes applications and cohort size, the observed base rate is used. Where it does not, the range is intentionally wider and lower-confidence. A programme's historical acceptance rate does not guarantee Pri the same rate.
>
> “Success” is defined separately for each row: funded selection, incubated cohort selection, signed pilot, accepted cloud-credit programme, or an equity term sheet. A reply, meeting or shortlist is not counted unless explicitly stated.
>
> **Eligibility overrides everything.** If a mandatory gate such as DPIIT recognition, revenue history, incorporation, full-time founder status, founder equity or programme age is not satisfied, the current success probability is **0% regardless of product quality**.

# 1. Pri-specific assumptions used in this model

The model assumes the following based on the current Pri project/repository state and project history:

## Positive factors

1. Pri has a substantial working product rather than a slide-deck-only concept.
2. The product is AI-native and directly relevant to Indian school education.
3. Pri's strongest potentially differentiated capability is **understanding/grading mathematical working and method**, including handwritten/multistep work, rather than merely returning a final answer.
4. Pri is curriculum-grounded and is being built for Indian secondary-school mathematics / NCERT-CBSE style learning.
5. The product already has meaningful engineering depth: authentication, grading, content, student journey, privacy/storage and release infrastructure have been built substantially beyond a prototype.
6. A young/student-founder profile can be an advantage in programmes such as Campus Fund and gradCapital if the formal eligibility/cap-table rules are satisfied.
7. Education AI remains strategically important in India: ShikshaNext launched in 2026 as a ₹170 crore multi-donor initiative, while government and major technology companies are explicitly pushing AI-enabled education.

## Negative factors

1. No independently verified **paid-school traction, paid consumer traction, externally measured learning outcomes, retention data or repeatable GTM evidence** has been identified in the current fundraising research package.
2. No DPIIT recognition or 12-month revenue evidence was found in the repository search used for this research. This is **not proof that Pri lacks them**; it means they must be verified before applying to programmes that require them.
3. The Indian AI-tutor market became materially more competitive in 2026.
4. Pri currently competes against incumbents with enormous content/data/distribution advantages and against new AI-native startups with experienced founders and significant funding.
5. Investors in Indian EdTech are more selective than in the 2021 boom. Tracxn-reported market coverage says 2026 funding is concentrated into fewer, larger rounds and investors increasingly demand sustainable models and proof.

Because of these factors, Pri's **product quality appears ahead of many application-stage startups**, but its **external proof is behind the strongest funded competitors**.

# 9. What moves the probabilities most

## Highest-leverage improvement 1 — measured learning outcomes

A well-run 8–12 week pilot showing meaningful improvement can approximately **double** Pri's odds at many evidence-sensitive investors/grants.

Required:
- predefined primary learning metric;
- baseline;
- post-test;
- comparison/control where practical;
- dropout accounting;
- confidence intervals / effect size where sample permits;
- no cherry-picking.

## Highest-leverage improvement 2 — retention

Investor narrative changes materially if Pri can show:
- D7 / D30;
- WAU/MAU;
- questions per active learner;
- voluntary return rate;
- week-4/week-8 cohort retention.

## Highest-leverage improvement 3 — benchmark the “method-aware” moat

Build a benchmark that tests:
- correct final answer but wrong reasoning;
- arithmetic slip inside valid method;
- equivalent symbolic forms;
- ambiguous handwriting;
- missing steps;
- alternate valid methods;
- misconception classification;
- partial-credit agreement with expert human markers.

If Pri beats strong baselines on this benchmark, it becomes substantially easier to explain why Pri is not just another chat interface.

## Highest-leverage improvement 4 — school reference

A respected school that is willing to say:
> “We deployed Pri with X students and saw Y”

is more valuable for the next funding round than another 20 product features.

## Highest-leverage improvement 5 — eligibility housekeeping

Immediately verify:
- Indian company structure;
- DPIIT status;
- founder equity;
- student/recent-graduate eligibility;
- founder full-time requirement;
- company age;
- 12-month revenue requirement;
- grant double-funding restrictions.

A brilliant application has **0%** chance if a hard gate is missed.

# 12. Decision rule

Do not interpret a 5% opportunity as “not worth applying.”

For a high-value programme:
- application cost may be a few founder-days;
- upside may be ₹20L–₹1Cr+, research credibility, or a transformative institutional relationship.

A rational fundraising strategy is therefore a **portfolio strategy**:
- pursue many independently valuable, highly matched opportunities;
- customise every application;
- avoid eligibility-invalid applications;
- generate evidence continuously;
- update the probabilities when real outcomes arrive.

The model should be recalculated after:
1. first paid customer;
2. first school pilot;
3. 100 active learners;
4. D30 retention data;
5. first externally measured learning-outcome study;
6. first institutional grant/investment;
7. any change in founder/company/DPIIT eligibility.
