# Transfer, Generalisation and Analogy Deep Dive V7

Status: V7 mechanistic deep dive
Freshness: 1 October 2026

## 1. Purpose

Pri's strongest learning claim should eventually be:

> the learner can use the mathematics when the surface form changes and Pri is no longer helping.

This is transfer.

Transfer is difficult.

It should not be inferred from:
- more practice;
- same-template variants;
- immediate post-test success.

## 2. Transfer is not one thing

Recommended Pri transfer taxonomy:

### T0 — same instance
Exact task repeated.

### T1 — same family, changed incidentals
Numbers/context changed.

### T2 — same construct, different family
Underlying mathematics shared, solution surface changed.

### T3 — strategy selection
Target method is not cued.

### T4 — representation shift
Symbolic ↔ graph ↔ table ↔ verbal ↔ geometry.

### T5 — composition
Known components must be coordinated in a new structure.

### T6 — contextual/modeling transfer
Mathematics must be recognized in a new real-world situation.

### T7 — cross-domain/far transfer
Much larger contextual/domain shift.

Claims should name the tier.

## 3. Near transfer is already difficult

A 2025 observational analysis used TIMSS 2019 data from roughly 280,000 students to test whether reported teaching practices aimed at schema formation/abstraction were associated with stronger performance on unfamiliar mathematics questions.

The authors did not find convincing evidence of such transfer associations.

Source:
https://doi.org/10.1016/j.lindif.2024.102609

Limitations:
- observational;
- teacher-report measures;
- cannot establish that schema-oriented instruction does not work.

### Pri consequence

Do not assume:
“we taught for understanding”
means transfer occurred.

Measure transfer directly.

## 4. Variability can help abstraction, but interacts with instruction

A 2026 two-experiment study crossed:
- retrieval practice vs worked examples;
- repeated vs varied items;
- initial instruction vs no initial instruction.

When learners had initial instruction:
retrieval practice supported stronger generalisation than worked examples in the studied tasks.

Without initial instruction:
worked examples were stronger than repeated retrieval, while varied retrieval performed comparably to worked examples.

Source:
https://doi.org/10.1007/s10648-026-10169-w

### Pri consequence

Variability is not a universally good add-on.

Its effect depends on:
- whether schema exists;
- whether learner must induce a rule;
- instructional support.

## 5. Surface variability versus structural variability

Changing:
- names;
- colors;
- numbers

may create surface variation only.

Structural variation changes:
- representation;
- solution route;
- operation order;
- irrelevant information;
- strategy cue;
- composition.

QuestionVariant should therefore record variation dimension.

## 6. Analogical comparison

Comparing two examples can help learners extract shared structure instead of focusing on surface details.

Mathematics research has used comparisons such as:
- two correct methods;
- correct vs incorrect;
- structurally similar problems.

A 2024 algebra study found English learners could benefit from worked-example comparison.

Source:
https://doi.org/10.1007/s11251-024-09668-6

### Pri consequence

Create a COMPARE intervention type rather than only single worked examples.

## 7. Analogies need mapping support

An analogy is not useful merely because two contexts seem similar.

A good analogy needs:
- relevant source known;
- structurally mapped relation;
- irrelevant surface differences controlled;
- learner attention directed to the mapping.

A 2026 mathematics commentary emphasizes that analogy quality requires explicit evaluation of conceptual affordance rather than intuition alone.

Source:
https://doi.org/10.1007/s42330-026-00502-4

## 8. Analogy can overload novices

Analogical comparison asks the learner to hold and map multiple representations.

For novices:
- worked example first;
- explicit mapping cue;
- limited dimensions.

For more advanced learners:
- learner-generated comparison;
- fewer cues.

This is another expertise-reversal candidate.

## 9. Grounded conceptual representations may support transfer

A 2025 experimental trigonometry study compared formal rule instruction with grounded conceptual instruction and reported stronger transfer under grounded conditions in the studied tasks.

Source:
https://doi.org/10.3389/fpsyg.2025.1507670

### Pri consequence

Pri Worlds / visual representations should not be treated as decoration.

They can be tested as tools for:
- conceptual grounding;
- later symbolic transfer.

But one study cannot establish a universal “visual is better” rule.

## 10. Real-world context can hurt or help

Context may:
- make meaning clearer;
- activate relevant prior knowledge.

It may also:
- add reading load;
- introduce unfamiliar cultural knowledge;
- obscure the mathematical structure.

Therefore contextual transfer should distinguish:
- mathematical difficulty;
- contextual familiarity.

## 11. Mathematical modelling transfer

A 2025 study with Grade 5/6 learners in Japan and Australia examined analogies across structurally similar modelling tasks and found that contextual and mathematical analogy use can contribute to knowledge transfer.

Source:
https://doi.org/10.1007/s11858-025-01675-2

Population/sample limitation:
small qualitative classroom study.

Use as mechanism evidence, not effect estimate.

## 12. Transfer requires source recognition

A learner may possess the relevant method but fail to notice that the new problem calls for it.

This means transfer includes at least:

1. recognize structure;
2. retrieve strategy;
3. adapt;
4. execute;
5. verify.

A failure at step 1 is different from skill loss.

Pri should diagnose strategy-recognition separately.

## 13. Interleaving as transfer preparation

Interleaving trains:
- discrimination among methods.

This is especially relevant to T3 strategy selection.

Blocked practice can produce excellent execution but weak method choice.

Thus:
- blocked execution evidence;
- interleaved strategy evidence

should be separate.

## 14. Representation transfer

Examples:

- equation → graph;
- graph → derivative sign;
- table → functional relationship;
- geometry diagram → symbolic proof.

A learner can be competent in one view and fragile in another.

Learner Evidence Graph should retain representation dimension.

## 15. Transfer-bank design

For each KnowledgeComponent:

### Practice bank
Can be used in ordinary learning.

### Validation bank
Used for capability benchmarking.

### Protected transfer bank
Unavailable to:
- tutoring retrieval;
- prompt examples;
- content generation training;
- direct practice.

Otherwise “transfer” leaks.

## 16. Family fingerprints

Transfer banks need structural fingerprints to reduce accidental sibling overlap.

Candidate features:
- solution graph;
- operations;
- strategy;
- representation;
- theorem/rule;
- context;
- answer form.

Similarity threshold should be validated.

## 17. Pre-specification

Before learner sees intervention:
- classify outcome transfer tier.

Do not inspect results and then call a successful question “far transfer.”

## 18. Delay and transfer are separate axes

A task can be:
- near transfer after 7 days;
- far transfer immediately.

Store:
- structural distance;
- temporal delay.

Both matter.

## 19. Assistance contamination

Transfer outcome should be independent unless the study explicitly examines supported transfer.

If a learner receives:
- strategic cue;
- translated problem;
- worked example

during outcome, label it.

Do not call it independent transfer.

## 20. External transfer

Board exam performance is a valuable external criterion because:
- item writers are outside Pri;
- format differs;
- content selection differs.

But board marks also include:
- test-day noise;
- broader curriculum;
- language;
- timing.

Use external assessment as one validity layer, not sole measure.

## 21. Learning by comparison intervention

Potential Pri flow:

Example A solution  
Example B solution  
→ “What is mathematically the same?”  
→ “What changes?”  
→ learner states rule  
→ new family item.

Measure:
- delayed new-family performance.

## 22. Incorrect comparison

Another powerful flow:

Correct solution vs plausible incorrect solution.

Ask:
- first divergence;
- invariant violated;
- repair.

This combines:
- analogy;
- erroneous examples;
- metacognition.

## 23. Transfer difficulty

Instead of one item difficulty, maintain:
- practice difficulty;
- transfer distance;
- context burden.

A T4 item can be easy numerically but high transfer.

## 24. Transfer-aware mastery

A learner state might say:

- strong within family;
- moderate family diversity;
- no representation transfer;
- delayed evidence due.

This is more truthful than:
“mastered 90%.”

## 25. Transfer experiments

### TR-1
Repeated siblings vs structurally varied practice.

### TR-2
Single worked example vs analogical comparison.

### TR-3
Blocked vs interleaved practice.

### TR-4
Symbolic-only vs linked representations.

### TR-5
Pri World interactive vs strong static representation.

### TR-6
Erroneous comparison vs standard correction.

Primary:
delayed held-out family/representation outcome.

## 26. Far transfer claims require exceptional caution

Across education research, far transfer is difficult and often weak.

Pri should prefer narrow honest claims:
- “transferred from symbolic to graphical representation”

rather than:
- “improved general problem-solving ability.”

General cognitive-transfer claims need substantially stronger evidence.

## 27. Transfer efficiency

Measure:
- transfer gain per minute;
- number of varied examples;
- cognitive load;
- learner burden.

More variability is not automatically better.

## 28. Transfer failure diagnosis

If learner fails a held-out item, possible causes:

- did not recognize structure;
- prerequisite missing;
- representation unfamiliar;
- context/language load;
- execution error;
- retention failure.

Transfer failure should create a diagnostic question, not instantly reduce every component estimate.

## 29. Teacher reporting

Useful:

> Solves standard linear-equation families independently but has not yet transferred the method to word-problem formulation.

This gives teacher action.

## 30. Core decision

Pri should treat transfer as an explicit measurement dimension with protected outcomes, structural-distance metadata and delayed independent verification.

The system should never infer:

> “many similar correct answers” = “generalized understanding.”
