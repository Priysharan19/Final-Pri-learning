# Motivation, Gamification and Mathematics Anxiety Deep Dive V7

Status: V7 mechanistic deep dive
Freshness: 1 October 2026

## 1. Purpose

Pri should help learners persist in mathematics.

But “engagement” is dangerous when treated as the goal.

A product can increase:
- opens;
- streaks;
- time;
- points;
- sessions

while reducing:
- autonomy;
- honest help-seeking;
- deep thought;
- independent learning.

This document separates:
- motivation;
- game mechanics;
- mathematics anxiety;
- engagement metrics.

## 2. Motivation has multiple forms

Self-Determination Theory distinguishes:

- autonomous/intrinsic motivation;
- controlled/extrinsic motivation;
- amotivation.

Important psychological needs:
- autonomy;
- competence;
- relatedness.

Pri should prefer:
- capability;
- ownership;
- meaningful progress

over:
- pressure;
- loss aversion;
- social comparison.

## 3. SDT interventions have empirical support

A 2024 systematic review/meta-analysis of 36 education interventions based on Self-Determination Theory found improvements in:
- intrinsic motivation;
- autonomy;
- competence.

Source:
https://doi.org/10.1016/j.lmot.2024.102015

This supports need-supportive design as a serious framework.

It does not give Pri a formula for motivation.

## 4. Gamification can improve motivation, but effects are heterogeneous

A 2026 mathematics-specific systematic review/meta-analysis found:
- 45 studies in qualitative review;
- 11 studies in meta-analysis;
- significant small-to-moderate improvement in motivation;
- g ≈ 0.383;
- substantial heterogeneity.

Source:
https://doi.org/10.1007/s10648-025-10108-1

Negative outcomes appeared in some contexts, especially:
- competition;
- social comparison;
- external rewards;
- poorly matched design.

### Pri consequence

Do not ask:

> Should Pri be gamified?

Ask:

> Which game mechanic supports which psychological need for which learner/task?

## 5. Broad gamified-learning evidence is positive but quality-sensitive

A 2026 second-order mathematics meta-analysis synthesized:
- 20 prior meta-analyses;
- 688 primary studies.

It reported:
- overall g ≈ 0.407;
- cognitive outcomes g ≈ 0.454;
- affective outcomes g ≈ 0.226 and not statistically significant;
- lower-quality studies tended to overestimate effects.

Source:
https://doi.org/10.1002/berj.70144

### Pri consequence

Do not treat “gamification works” as established product guidance.

The learning effect appears more robust than affective claims in this synthesis.

Quality matters.

## 6. Another 2026 second-order review reaches a similar boundary

A second-order review of nine mathematics game-based-learning meta-analyses reported:
- overall positive but modest effect;
- ES ≈ 0.45;
- high heterogeneity;
- cognitive outcomes more consistent than affective outcomes.

Source:
https://doi.org/10.1016/j.edurev.2026.100816

The convergence is useful.

But second-order meta-analysis inherits:
- overlap;
- varying definitions;
- primary-study weaknesses.

Use as direction, not exact expected effect.

## 7. Gamification can support autonomy and relatedness without guaranteeing competence

A 2024 meta-analysis of 35 gamification interventions found:
- small intrinsic-motivation effect;
- positive autonomy effect;
- large relatedness effect;
- relatively small competence effect.

Source:
https://doi.org/10.1007/s11423-023-10337-7

### Pri consequence

A social/choice mechanic can feel motivating without improving mathematics.

Always pair motivation metrics with learning outcomes.

## 8. K–12 motivation effects can be heavily extrinsic

A 2025 K–12 meta-analysis found positive motivation effects with high heterogeneity, and effects on extrinsic motivation were at least as large as those on intrinsic motivation.

Source:
https://doi.org/10.1002/pits.70056

### Pri consequence

Points/badges can produce short-run participation.

Do not assume this means durable autonomous motivation.

## 9. Mechanic taxonomy

### Competence-supporting
- progress evidence;
- skill unlock based on real capability;
- recoverable challenge;
- specific feedback.

### Autonomy-supporting
- meaningful task choice;
- explanation depth choice;
- goal selection;
- defer/override.

### Relatedness-supporting
- structured collaboration;
- teacher acknowledgement;
- cooperative goals.

### Controlled-pressure mechanics
- punitive streaks;
- public rank;
- scarcity;
- loss framing;
- shame.

Pri should default toward the first three.

## 10. Streaks

Potential benefit:
- routine cue;
- visible continuity.

Potential harm:
- studying to protect streak;
- anxiety;
- fake/minimal sessions;
- shame after break.

Recommended design:
- flexible;
- recoverable;
- not tied to self-worth;
- optional;
- never a mastery signal.

## 11. Reward currency

Points should not become the product's main economy.

If used:
- reward meaningful learning actions;
- avoid paying for answer volume;
- avoid encouraging spam.

Better reward:
- “independent recovery achieved.”

Worse:
- “+10 points for opening Pri.”

## 12. Leaderboards

Risks:
- lower-performing learners disengage;
- social comparison;
- strategic gaming;
- privacy.

The 2026 mathematics gamification review specifically reports negative cases involving competitive/performance-oriented designs.

Default:
no public leaderboard.

If tested:
- opt-in;
- team/cooperative forms;
- lower-tail guardrails.

## 13. Challenge

Motivation depends partly on perceived competence.

Too easy:
- boredom.

Too hard:
- threat/helplessness.

But Pri must not infer an exact universal “optimal challenge percentage.”

Use:
- learner evidence;
- failure/recovery;
- abandonment;
- experiments.

## 14. Mathematics anxiety is not the same as low skill

A learner can:
- know mathematics and feel anxious;
- lack skill without high anxiety;
- experience both.

Keep separate constructs.

## 15. Mathematics anxiety intervention meta-analysis

A 2026 meta-analysis included:
- 51 studies;
- 7,673 participants;
- children through adults.

Reported anxiety reductions:
- combined skills + anxiety interventions strongest in raw subgroup estimates;
- anxiety-focused interventions next;
- skills-focused interventions also reduced anxiety.

For mathematics performance:
- only mathematics-skills-oriented interventions showed an effective improvement in the reviewed outcomes.

Source:
https://doi.org/10.1037/edu0000992

### Pri consequence

Pri's primary role is mathematics learning.

Improving competence can itself reduce anxiety.

Do not turn Pri into an anxiety-treatment app.

## 16. Anxiety-specific support boundary

Safe product strategies:
- private low-stakes practice;
- predictable feedback;
- no public rank;
- learner-controlled pacing;
- clear recovery after mistakes;
- reduce unnecessary time pressure;
- show competence evidence accurately.

Avoid:
- diagnosing anxiety disorder;
- therapy-like claims;
- psychological treatment without appropriate clinical scope.

## 17. Timed modes

Timing can:
- build fluency;
- create exam realism.

But can also:
- increase threat;
- obscure actual reasoning ability.

Use timed practice only when:
- fluency/time pressure is relevant to the target;
- learner knows why;
- untimed evidence remains available.

## 18. Progress feedback

Good progress feedback:

> You solved three different factorization families independently this week. A delayed check is still due.

Bad:

> 87% smarter!

Progress should reflect real evidence.

## 19. Competence feedback

Specific informational feedback supports competence better than generic praise.

Prefer:
- “You chose the method without a hint and recovered the sign error yourself.”

Over:
- “Amazing! You're a genius!”

Avoid identity praise.

## 20. Autonomy

Meaningful learner controls:
- topic selection;
- goal;
- assistance level;
- session length;
- language;
- practice/exam mode.

But Pri can still recommend.

Recommended:
- explain why;
- allow override.

## 21. Relatedness

Relatedness does not require an anthropomorphic AI relationship.

Sources:
- teacher;
- peers;
- family support;
- cooperative PairLab.

Pri itself should remain a transparent tool, not a simulated dependent friend.

## 22. Session stopping

A product that maximizes time-in-app is misaligned.

Pri should sometimes recommend:

> That's enough for today. Your next useful review is tomorrow.

This protects:
- fatigue;
- autonomy;
- learning efficiency.

## 23. Engagement metrics

Separate:

### Behavioral
- starts;
- completion;
- time.

### Cognitive
- meaningful attempts;
- explanations;
- verification.

### Emotional/self-report
- interest;
- value;
- anxiety.

### Learning
- delayed independent outcome.

Behavioral engagement is not a proxy for learning.

## 24. Game mechanic admission

Each mechanic should state:

- target mechanism;
- target population;
- expected benefit;
- possible harm;
- learning metric;
- autonomy guardrail.

Example:

Mechanic:
flexible streak.

Mechanism:
habit cue.

Primary metric:
return rate + delayed practice completion.

Guardrail:
no increased rushed/minimal sessions or stress.

## 25. Motivation experiments

### MOT-1
Flexible streak vs no streak.

### MOT-2
Capability-based progress vs points.

### MOT-3
Learner-selected goals vs system-only goals.

### MOT-4
Cooperative PairLab goal vs competitive rank.

### MOT-5
Threat-reducing error language.

Primary outcomes:
- voluntary quality practice;
- delayed learning;
- autonomy/interest where measured.

## 26. Do not personalize motivation from personality inference

Avoid:
- “competitive learner”;
- “needs praise”;
- “low grit.”

Personalization should use explicit preference or intervention-response evidence.

## 27. Core decision

Pri should optimize motivation by making genuine mathematical competence visible, preserving meaningful learner control, minimizing shame and social pressure, and testing game mechanics as interventions.

The goal is:

> a learner who increasingly wants and knows how to learn mathematics even when Pri is not trying to pull them back into the app.
