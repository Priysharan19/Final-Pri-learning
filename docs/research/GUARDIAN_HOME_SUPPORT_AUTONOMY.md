# Guardian, Home Support and Learner Autonomy Architecture

Status: **research-to-product contract**.  
Freshness: **1 October 2026**.

## 1. Guardian involvement is not automatically beneficial

A mathematics-specific meta-analysis of 20 studies / 41 effects / 16,338 participants found:

- supportive parental homework involvement had a small positive association with mathematics achievement;
- intrusive involvement had a negative association;
- autonomy support showed the strongest positive relationship among supportive subtypes.

Source:
https://doi.org/10.3389/fpsyg.2023.1218534

A broader three-level meta-analysis similarly found autonomy support was the parental-homework dimension most consistently positively related to achievement.

Source:
https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/38227295/

This is primarily correlational evidence.

Pri should not claim its guardian features cause higher achievement without experiments.

## 2. Product objective

Guardian support should increase:

- availability of practical support;
- learner autonomy;
- constructive encouragement;
- stable study environment.

It should not increase:

- surveillance;
- shame;
- forced overpractice;
- minute-by-minute monitoring;
- conflict.

## 3. Permission separation

Separate:

- legal/guardian consent;
- account administration;
- purchase authority;
- learning visibility;
- raw artifact visibility;
- tutor transcript visibility;
- research participation;
- notifications.

One role does not imply all permissions.

## 4. Student autonomy boundary

Where age/jurisdiction/policy allows, the learner should have a meaningful private learning space.

Guardian dashboard need not expose:

- every wrong answer;
- every hint request;
- every confidence rating;
- every tutor conversation;
- every handwritten page.

High-level learning support can be enough.

## 5. Guardian digest

Prefer:

- current goals;
- broad topic progress;
- planned assessment dates;
- consistent practice summary;
- supportive suggested action;
- system uncertainty.

Avoid:

- “your child failed 17 questions today”;
- rank/shame;
- opaque risk scores;
- “they asked for 6 hints” alerts.

## 6. Supportive action library

Candidate suggestions:

- ask the learner what they want help with;
- create quiet study time;
- encourage explaining a method;
- celebrate recovery/effort evidence;
- contact teacher when repeated uncertainty persists.

Avoid directing guardians to teach mathematics beyond their confidence.

## 7. Intrusive-support risk

Longitudinal research has linked intrusive/uninvited homework support with worse achievement patterns, particularly for some learners.

Source:
https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/37166869/

This reinforces the principle:

> Pri should not turn guardians into remote proctors.

## 8. Notifications

Notifications should be:
- low frequency;
- actionable;
- non-shaming;
- learner-respecting.

Examples:
- upcoming goal requested by learner;
- account/security;
- teacher-approved event;
- broad weekly digest.

Avoid real-time “mistake alerts.”

## 9. Goal setting

Guardian and learner goals can differ.

Pri should make explicit:
- learner goal;
- guardian-supported goal;
- teacher/curriculum requirement.

Do not silently replace the learner's goal with the adult's.

## 10. Shared-device homes

Guardian controls must not create cross-sibling leakage.

On one household device:
- profiles isolated;
- guardian sees only authorized learners;
- queued events remain scoped;
- notification previews avoid sensitive details.

## 11. Financial authority

Purchase/subscription authority is separate from learning visibility.

A guardian who pays does not automatically require access to raw learning data beyond policy.

## 12. Teacher–guardian boundary

Pri should not automatically send machine-inferred concerns to guardians.

High-impact communication may require:
- teacher review;
- sufficient evidence;
- appropriate policy;
- student/guardian visibility rules.

## 13. Progress representation

Show evidence honestly:

- “independent on two families”;
- “still using hints”;
- “not checked after a delay”;
- “insufficient evidence.”

Avoid one performance score that invites harmful comparison.

## 14. Sibling/peer comparison

Do not offer family leaderboards by default.

Different curricula, age, evidence volume and learning profiles make comparison misleading and potentially harmful.

## 15. Guardian experiments

If testing guardian interventions, measure:

- learner autonomy;
- help-seeking honesty;
- conflict/stress where ethically collected;
- practice quality;
- delayed learning;
- guardian burden.

Do not optimize:
- dashboard opens;
- messages sent;
- minutes monitored.

## 16. Learner visibility into guardian data

Where policy permits, learner should understand:

- what guardians can see;
- what is private;
- why visibility changed.

Avoid hidden surveillance.

## 17. Age transitions

Permissions should be versioned across age/jurisdiction transitions.

When learner gains greater autonomy:
- update visibility;
- preserve legal requirements;
- explain changes.

Do not keep childhood sharing defaults forever.

## 18. High-risk situations

Guardian product is not a clinical/safeguarding diagnostic system.

If safety policy requires escalation, follow dedicated safety/legal process.

Do not infer serious risk from ordinary mathematics behavior.

## 19. Core rule

> **Guardian features should make it easier to support the learner, not easier to control the learner.**
