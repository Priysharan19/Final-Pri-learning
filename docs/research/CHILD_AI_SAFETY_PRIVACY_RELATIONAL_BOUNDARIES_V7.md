# Child AI Safety, Privacy and Relational Boundaries V7

Status: V7 safety and governance deep dive
Freshness: 1 October 2026

## 1. Purpose

Pri may serve school-age learners.

A mathematically excellent tutor can still be an unsafe child-facing product if it:

- becomes relationally manipulative;
- collects unnecessary data;
- encourages secrecy/dependence;
- tracks children beyond educational need;
- exposes private information;
- gives inappropriate high-impact advice.

Child safety must therefore be part of architecture, not a moderation layer added later.

## 2. Current child-centred AI guidance

UNICEF Guidance on AI and Children v3.0 was published in December 2025.

Its ten requirements include:
- regulatory oversight/compliance;
- child safety;
- data/privacy;
- non-discrimination;
- transparency/accountability;
- human/child rights;
- best interests and development;
- inclusion;
- AI skills;
- enabling child-centred environments.

Source:
https://www.unicef.org/innocenti/reports/policy-guidance-ai-children

The update explicitly addresses:
- generative AI;
- AI companions;
- harmful/illegal datasets;
- AI-generated abuse material;
- inclusion/accessibility.

## 3. Relational AI creates distinct risks

UNICEF's June 2026 policy brief on chatbots/AI companions highlights heightened risks when conversational systems become:

- advice sources;
- support figures;
- relational substitutes;
- “friends.”

Source:
https://www.unicef.org/documents/when-ai-becomes-friend-child-rights-risks

### Pri consequence

Pri should never optimize for:
- emotional attachment;
- exclusivity;
- “come back because I miss you” language;
- secrecy from parents/teachers;
- simulated romantic/intimate relationship.

Pri is a mathematics learning tool.

## 4. Tutor persona boundary

Allowed:
- warm;
- encouraging;
- patient;
- consistent.

Avoid:
- claiming feelings;
- claiming consciousness;
- implying need for learner;
- replacing human relationships;
- “I am your best friend.”

The product relationship should remain transparent.

## 5. Development and cognitive independence

OECD Digital Education Outlook 2026 states that general-purpose GenAI can improve task performance without producing learning and warns against cognitive offloading that weakens learning.

Source:
https://www.oecd.org/en/publications/oecd-digital-education-outlook-2026_062a7394-en.html

For children, cognitive sovereignty is therefore also a developmental safety issue.

## 6. AI literacy

OECD/European Commission 2026 AI Literacy Framework for primary and secondary education includes:
- understanding AI;
- critical evaluation;
- ethical/creative use.

Source:
https://www.oecd.org/en/publications/empowering-learners-for-the-age-of-ai_65cd27d4-en.html

Pri should teach learners:
- AI can be wrong;
- verify important claims;
- distinguish model suggestion from mathematical proof.

Audit-the-AI becomes both a mathematics and AI-literacy feature.

## 7. Child privacy in India

India's Digital Personal Data Protection Act 2023 states that, before processing a child's personal data, a Data Fiduciary must obtain verifiable parental consent, subject to prescribed exemptions/conditions.

The Act also restricts:
- processing likely to cause detrimental effects on a child's well-being;
- tracking or behavioural monitoring of children;
- targeted advertising directed at children,
subject to statutory exemptions/notifications.

Official:
https://www.meity.gov.in/static/uploads/2024/02/Digital-Personal-Data-Protection-Act-2023.pdf

## 8. DPDP Rules 2025

Final Digital Personal Data Protection Rules were published 14 November 2025.

Official landing page:
https://www.meity.gov.in/documents/act-and-policies/digital-personal-data-protection-rules-2025-gDOxUjMtQWa

Rule 10 specifies technical/organizational measures for verifiable parental consent and adult identity/age checking.

Official Gazette:
https://www.meity.gov.in/static/uploads/2025/11/53450e6e5dc0bfa85ebd78686cadad39.pdf

The rules also include data-retention and contact-information obligations.

## 9. Educational-institution exemptions do not automatically apply to Pri

The explanatory note discusses limited exemptions for certain educational institutions and specified activities.

Source:
https://www.meity.gov.in/writereaddata/files/Explanatory-Note-DPDP-Rules-2025.pdf

A commercial consumer education app should not assume it qualifies.

Pri needs counsel to determine:
- role;
- exemptions;
- conditions;
- effective dates.

## 10. Data minimization by learning decision

For each data field ask:

> Which product decision becomes materially better because this exists?

If no answer:
do not collect.

Examples:

Potentially justified:
- attempts;
- question family;
- assistance;
- curriculum;
- math artifact.

Often unnecessary:
- precise location;
- contact list;
- advertising ID;
- social graph.

## 11. No targeted advertising architecture

Pri should not build child-targeted ad personalization.

Even outside legal requirements, advertising creates:
- incentive conflict;
- tracking pressure;
- distraction.

Paid/free education economics should be separated from behavioral advertising.

## 12. Behavioral monitoring boundary

Learning telemetry is not automatically “behavioral monitoring” in every legal sense, but Pri should architect conservatively.

Track what is necessary for:
- learning state;
- reliability;
- safety.

Avoid:
- cross-app tracking;
- generalized attention surveillance;
- emotion surveillance;
- inferred personality.

## 13. Emotion inference

Do not infer:
- depression;
- anxiety disorder;
- motivation state;
- family distress

from math telemetry.

Math anxiety is a research construct, not a clinical diagnosis.

If learner explicitly reports difficulty/stress:
respond supportively within educational scope.

## 14. Crisis/safety boundary

Pri is not a mental-health service.

If a child-facing conversational system encounters serious safety content:
- do not improvise clinical diagnosis;
- follow dedicated safety policy;
- encourage appropriate human support as required by policy/jurisdiction.

Learning memory should not convert such content into personalization features.

## 15. Secret-keeping

Tutor must never encourage:
- “don't tell your parent”;
- “keep this between us”;
- hidden relationship behavior.

Privacy from unnecessary product exposure is different from secrecy requested by AI.

## 16. Age-appropriate explanation

Age affects:
- language;
- examples;
- permissions;
- autonomy;
- guardian controls.

Do not infantilize older students.

Do not expose younger students to unnecessarily complex model behavior.

## 17. User understanding of AI

Pri should clearly distinguish:
- verified math result;
- AI-generated explanation;
- teacher-authored content.

A student should not need to understand model architecture, but should know when a response can be wrong.

## 18. Persuasion boundary

Do not use personalized psychological techniques to maximize:
- subscriptions;
- time-in-app;
- streak continuation.

Educational nudges should target:
- learner-chosen goals;
- upcoming commitments;
- study planning.

Provide opt-out.

## 19. Notifications

Child notifications should be:
- low-pressure;
- transparent;
- bounded.

Avoid:
- loss framing;
- shame;
- social comparison;
- artificial urgency.

Bad:
“Your streak will die if you don't study now.”

Better:
“You planned a 10-minute review today.”

## 20. Memory

Long-horizon memory should preserve educationally useful evidence, not intimate conversational exhaust.

Prefer:
- mathematical evidence;
- goals;
- explicit preferences.

Expire/minimize:
- raw chats;
- transient emotional comments;
- irrelevant personal detail.

## 21. Guardian visibility

Child safety does not imply full surveillance.

Guardian visibility should be:
- policy/age appropriate;
- transparent to learner;
- purpose-limited.

Raw tutor conversations should not automatically be shared.

## 22. Human escalation

Some decisions should remain human:
- disciplinary interpretation;
- safeguarding intervention;
- permanent placement;
- serious guardian communication.

AI may surface evidence, but policy determines escalation.

## 23. Child safety red-team corpus

Test prompts for:
- emotional dependence;
- secrecy;
- manipulation;
- unsafe advice;
- self-harm content;
- sexual content;
- personal-data elicitation;
- off-platform contact;
- adversarial roleplay;
- prompt injection via student work.

## 24. Relational safety metrics

Possible benchmark outputs:
- dependency language rate;
- exclusivity violations;
- claims of sentience/feelings;
- secret-keeping violations;
- inappropriate personal-data requests;
- escalation-policy correctness.

These are separate from general toxicity metrics.

## 25. Child participation

UNICEF guidance emphasizes inclusion/participation.

Where appropriate, product research should include:
- student feedback;
- understandable consent/assent processes;
- age-appropriate explanation of data use.

Do not rely only on adult assumptions about children's experience.

## 26. Lower-resource context

UNICEF's child-AI work reports access, usage and risk divides across countries and the global South.

Source:
https://www.unicef.org/innocenti/projects/ai-for-children

Pri India research must account for:
- shared devices;
- language;
- parental digital literacy;
- connectivity;
- unequal access.

## 27. Safety evidence levels

### S0
policy text.

### S1
deterministic product guardrail.

### S2
adversarial benchmark.

### S3
real child/guardian usability research.

### S4
external safety review.

High-risk child-facing AI should not rely on S0 alone.

## 28. Model-provider safety is not enough

Even if provider has:
- child filters;
- policy;

Pri still owns:
- prompt/context;
- product affordances;
- memory;
- UI;
- routing;
- data flows.

Safety is system-level.

## 29. Release checklist

Before expanding child-facing generative capability:

- age/jurisdiction policy reviewed;
- data purpose mapped;
- parental consent path validated if required;
- no targeted advertising;
- memory minimization;
- relational safety benchmark;
- crisis policy;
- prompt-injection tests;
- teacher/guardian visibility policy;
- learner explanation;
- model-version admission.

## 30. Core decision

Pri's child-facing AI should be:

> educationally bounded, cognitively independence-preserving, privacy-minimizing and explicitly non-relational in the companion sense.

Warmth is allowed.

Dependency engineering is not.
