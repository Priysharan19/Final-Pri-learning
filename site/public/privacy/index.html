Privacy notice

Last updated: 9 November 2026

This notice explains what Pri Learning collects, why, and what you can ask us to do about it. It is written for students and parents in India, and it is meant to give you what India's Digital Personal Data Protection Act 2023 asks a notice to give you. It has not yet been reviewed by a lawyer; the app says so above this notice, and will stop saying so only when that review has happened.

The person responsible for your data (the "data fiduciary") is Priysharan Tripathi, 5 Juniper Pl, Oakville NSW 2765, Australia.

The short version

You need a Pri Learning account, and a connection, to have answers checked. Marking is done online by our server: it issues each question, and its marking engine — a fixed set of mathematical rules, not an AI model — decides the mark for a signed-in account. A copy of that engine runs on your device only to show your notation as you write and to display results recorded by earlier versions of the app; it never sets the mark for a new answer. Signed out or offline, nothing is checked or marked: the work you type or write is kept on your device until it can be checked.

Your handwriting and photos are read online. When you write an answer by hand or attach a photo, a picture of it is sent to our server, which passes it to OpenAI to read. This is on for a signed-in account and can be turned off, in which case you type your answers instead. The first time, the app asks your permission, naming OpenAI, and nothing is sent until you agree. A second setting, off unless you turn it on, sends your working to the same service to find where a mistake is. Both are described below.

Without an account you can open the app and look around, but nothing about your learning is sent anywhere — and nothing is marked.

Where your data is stored

  • Our server runs on Railway, a hosting provider. On 4 October 2026 the production service and its database volume were in Railway's ams region (Amsterdam, the Netherlands). Until the planned move described next, your account data is stored there, not in India.
  • Our database is planned to move to Supabase in its Mumbai region (ap-south-1). This notice will be updated when that move has happened; until then, do not read it as saying your data is stored in India.
  • OpenAI reads handwriting, photos and (if you turn it on) your working outside India.
  • Our email delivery provider and the app stores process what they need in their own regions.

What stays on your device

  • Your profile: the name you typed, your class or track, and your settings.
  • A copy of every question you have answered, your answers, your marks and your history. The marks themselves are decided by our server.
  • Your handwriting strokes.
  • Your streaks, badges and progress.

If you set a password on a profile, this data is encrypted at rest on the device. A profile without a password is not encrypted. Whoever can unlock the device can open a profile that has no password.

What we receive if you create a cloud account

  • Your email address, to sign you in and to send verification and password reset links and one-time sign-in codes. We do not offer sign-in by SMS; if we ever do, this notice will describe it first.
  • Your name, as you typed it.
  • Your answers, for marking: the question our server issued, the answer and working you submit, and the mark the server decided; your exam papers and results.
  • Learning events: that you answered a question, which chapter it belonged to, whether you were right, and how long you took.

  • A picture of your handwriting, when you are signed in. Your strokes stay on your device. Handwriting is read online: a picture of your writing is sent to our server and read there. This is on by default for a signed-in account and you can turn it off at any time in Settings → Handwriting, after which you type your answers; it stays off for an account under 18 until a parent or guardian has confirmed it, and it never runs without an account.

    Nothing is sent until you agree. The first time a picture would be sent — your handwriting, a photo of your working or a photo of a question — the app asks your permission once, naming OpenAI. If you say no, reading is turned off and you type your answers; if you turn it back on, you are asked again before the next picture. Your answer is kept on your device, for that profile, and the app asks again whenever what it tells you changes.

    When you write with a pencil or your finger, what is sent is an image drawn from your own stroke coordinates and nothing else: not the question, not the expected answer, not your name or profile.

    The same setting also covers a photograph. If you attach a photo of work done on paper, or a scanned PDF of it, that photograph is sent to be read by the same service. It is your camera's picture, resized and re-encoded but not otherwise altered — so whatever is in the frame is sent, which can include the printed question, a name written on the page, or anything else on the paper. Keep out of shot whatever you would not want sent, or type your working instead.

    Either way, our server passes it to an outside reading service — OpenAI, through its API — and keeps no copy of the picture: it is not written to our database, our storage or our logs. What we do keep is the text the reading service returned, and any correction you typed, for 90 days with the record of that answer. We ask the reading service not to store it. Under its own terms it does not use it to train its models, but it may hold a copy for a limited time to watch for abuse; we do not control that. Turning the setting off stops the next picture of your handwriting or working from being sent, and it stays off; a picture already sent cannot be recalled. "Practise this" is separate: a photo of a printed question, taken to find practice questions like it, goes to OpenAI the same way after the same one-time permission, but the Handwriting setting does not cover it. It is sent only when you use "Practise this". What the reading service returns is a transcription of your writing, nothing more, and you can correct it before it counts: your mark is always decided by the marking engine on our server, never by the reading service.

  • Your working and the question, only if you switch that on. There is a second setting, also off unless you turn it on, that sends the lines of working you wrote and the text of the question when an answer is wrong and the app cannot tell you which line went wrong. It is used to tell you where the mistake is. Our server keeps no copy; the checking service handles it as the reading service above does. The expected answer is never sent with it, and your name and profile are never sent with it. Your mark is decided by the marking engine on our server either way.
  • A parent or guardian's name and email address, only for an account of someone under 18, so we can send them the confirmation link or code described under Children and show you, partly hidden, where it went.
  • Device information needed to keep you signed in: a random device identifier this app generates (it is not your device's own id and not anything you typed), a one-way hash of your browser's user-agent string, and the times you signed in and were last seen.
  • Subscription status from the App Store or Google Play, if you subscribe: whether a subscription is active, which plan, and when the paid period ends. Never your card details.
  • Crash reports, while the Crash reports setting is on (it is on for a signed-in account and can be turned off for the device in Settings): the name of the screen that failed, a short error code, a fingerprint of the fault and the app's build number. Never the error message, the page address, the question, your writing or anything you typed.

Children

Pri Learning is used by children. Under the DPDP Act, a child is anyone under 18, and a child's data may only be processed with verifiable consent from a parent or guardian.

What the app does today, plainly: it does not ask for your age or date of birth. It asks which class you are in, which tells it what maths to set you. When you create a cloud account it asks whether you are 18 or older, and unless you say you are, the account is treated as a child's. It then asks for a parent or guardian's name and email address. We email them a link to confirm the account, or a six-digit code, which they enter on their own page, on their own device, after reading a short notice of what they are agreeing to. Until they confirm, the account can sign in, show and export what it holds, and be deleted, and nothing else: no answer is marked, no handwriting or photo is read, and it does not sync, join a class, send a report, send a crash report, subscribe, or use either server setting. The same email lets them withdraw at any time, which stops all of that at once — marking, reading, exams and sync; a withdrawn confirmation cannot be given again with the same link.

If no parent or guardian has confirmed the account 30 days after it was created, or 30 days after a parent or guardian withdraws, the account is closed: it stops working and every sign-in on it ends. Everything it holds is erased 7 days later. A parent or guardian can also ask us to erase the account without the child's login, from the link in our email or by writing to the address under Your rights; it then stops working at once and is erased 7 days later.

What that confirmation proves, and what it does not. It shows that someone who can read that email inbox followed the link or entered the code. It does not show that they are an adult, or that they are your parent or guardian, so it is not the verifiable parental consent the DPDP Act will require. Our record of it says which method was used, so nobody can mistake it for more than it is.

If you are under 18, please set this up with a parent or guardian and show them this notice, particularly the section on what a cloud account sends. Answers cannot be checked without an account; without one, nothing about your learning leaves the device.

The DPDP Act's obligations on children's data, including verifiable parental consent for anyone under 18, are expected to take effect in May 2027, eighteen months after the DPDP Rules were notified in November 2025. Before then, and before this app is offered to children at scale, the email confirmation has to be replaced by a method that does verify the parent or guardian, and this section rewritten to describe it.

We do not use children's data for advertising, we do not track children across other services, and we do not build behavioural profiles for anything other than choosing the next maths question.

Who else sees your data

Only the services needed to run the parts you use:

  • Email delivery (Resend) for verification links, password reset links, one-time codes and the confirmation sent to a parent or guardian. The delivery service sees the address and the message, as a postal service sees an envelope.
  • Apple and Google, if you subscribe through the App Store or Google Play. We never see or store your card details; the store tells us only whether a subscription is active, which plan, and until when.
  • Our hosting and database providers (Railway; Supabase after the planned move), which store and process it for us — see "Where your data is stored".
  • A handwriting reader (OpenAI), while the handwriting-reading setting is on and you have agreed to send it, and only the image of your writing or the photo described above.
  • A step checker (the same provider), only while the optional working-check setting is on, and only the working and question described above.

We do not sell your data. We do not share it for advertising.

How long we keep it

  • Data on your device stays until you delete the profile or the app.
  • Learning events on our server are kept while your account is open.
  • The record of each marked answer — the question, your answer and working, the transcription of your handwriting or photo with any correction you typed, and the mark — is kept for 90 days. After that your answer, your working and the transcription are gone; which question it was, whether it was right, the marks and the chapter stay in your learning history while your account is open.
  • Exam papers, the answers you gave in them and their results are kept for up to five years, or until you delete your account, whichever comes first.
  • Telemetry, including crash reports, is limited to an allow-list of fields and is deleted after 90 days.
  • An account of someone under 18 that no parent or guardian has confirmed 30 days after it was created, or 30 days after a parent or guardian withdrew, is closed then and erased 7 days later, as described under Children.
  • When you delete your account it is deleted at once — there is no waiting period. That removes your profile, your sign-in details and every session, your synced work and answer records, your exam papers, your class memberships and submissions, your subscription link, your telemetry and any guardian's details. Your email address can then be used to create a new, empty account. Deleting the account does not cancel a subscription in the App Store or Google Play; cancel it there, or it will renew.
  • Three things are kept after deletion, with nothing in them that identifies you: the record of any payment you made (amount, currency, date and the store's own reference), which tax and accounting law requires us to keep; any problem report you sent about a question, with everything you typed into it removed; and a log entry saying that an account was deleted, with no name or email.
  • Backups of our database made before you deleted your account still contain it until they are replaced. Operational logs that contain no personal data may persist.

Your rights

You can ask us to:

  • Show you what we hold about you. The app exports it directly.
  • Correct anything that is wrong.
  • Delete your account and its data. You can do this inside the app, in Settings → Account, whichever way you sign in: with your password, with your Apple or Google sign-in, or with a code sent to your email address. If you no longer have the app, you can do it at /account/delete-request with a code sent to the account's email address. Either way the deletion is immediate, as described under "How long we keep it".
  • Withdraw consent, after which we stop processing and delete.
  • Nominate someone to exercise these rights if you cannot.

Write to impriysharan@icloud.com. See Grievances for how complaints are handled and how to escalate to the Data Protection Board of India.

Security

Passwords are stored only as bcrypt hashes. Sign-in tokens are stored as one-way hashes. Verification and reset links are single-use, short-lived, and delivered encrypted. One-time codes are never stored — only a keyed one-way hash of each is — and they expire after ten minutes and five attempts. Connections use HTTPS.

No system is perfectly secure. If we discover a breach affecting your data, we will tell you and the Data Protection Board as the DPDP Act requires.
