Privacy notice

Last updated: 18 November 2026

This notice explains what Pri Learning collects, why, and what you can ask us to do about it. It is written for students and parents in India, and it is meant to give you what India's Digital Personal Data Protection Act 2023 asks a notice to give you. It has not yet been reviewed by a lawyer; the app says so above this notice, and will stop saying so only when that review has happened.

The person responsible for your data (the "data fiduciary") is Priysharan Tripathi trading as Pri Learning, ABN 20 192 090 394.

The short version

You need a Pri Learning account, and a connection, to have answers checked. Marking is done online by our server: it issues each question, and its marking engine — a fixed set of mathematical rules, not an AI model — decides the mark for a signed-in account. A copy of that engine runs on your device only to show your notation as you write and to display results recorded by earlier versions of the app; it never sets the mark for a new answer. Signed out or offline, nothing is checked or marked: the work you type or write is kept on your device until it can be checked.

Your handwriting and photos are read online. When you write an answer by hand or attach a photo, a picture of it is sent to our server, which passes it to OpenAI to read. This is on for a signed-in account and can be turned off, in which case you type your answers instead. The first time, the app asks your permission, naming OpenAI, and nothing is sent until you agree. A second setting, off unless you turn it on, sends your working to the same service to find where a mistake is. Both are described below.

Your original handwriting is kept with your marks. When you submit a handwritten or photographed practice answer to be marked, we keep your original handwriting or photo with the mark for 2 years, and then delete it automatically. You can get it in your data export. Only you, and your class teacher for work you hand in, can ever be allowed to open it. The app has no screen that shows it yet. See "Your original handwriting and photos" below.

Without an account you can open the app and look around, but nothing about your learning is sent anywhere — and nothing is marked.

Where your data is stored

  • Our server runs on Railway, a hosting provider, in its Amsterdam region (EU West, the Netherlands).
  • Our database, which holds your account and learning records, is held on Neon (running on AWS), in its Singapore region (ap-southeast-1). Your account data is stored there, not in India.
  • OpenAI reads handwriting, photos and (if you turn it on) your working outside India.
  • Our email delivery provider and the app stores process what they need in their own regions.

What stays on your device

  • Your profile: the name you typed, your class or track, and your settings.
  • A copy of every question you have answered, your answers, your marks and your history. The marks themselves are decided by our server.
  • Your handwriting strokes. (When an answer is marked, its original is also kept on our server: see "Your original handwriting and photos".)
  • Your streaks, badges and progress.

If you set a password on a profile, this data is encrypted at rest on the device. A profile without a password is not encrypted. Whoever can unlock the device can open a profile that has no password.

What we receive if you create a cloud account

  • Who invited whom, if you use an invite link: we keep a record that your account joined through another account's link (or that yours was used), your invite code, and the dates of the free trial it gave. We never ask for or keep a friend's email address or phone number; you share the link yourself.
  • Your email address, to sign you in and to send verification and password reset links and one-time sign-in codes. We do not offer sign-in by SMS; if we ever do, this notice will describe it first.
  • Your name, as you typed it.
  • Your answers, for marking: the question our server issued, the answer and working you submit, and the mark the server decided; your exam papers and results.
  • Learning events: that you answered a question, which chapter it belonged to, whether you were right, and how long you took.

  • A picture of your handwriting, when you are signed in. Your strokes stay on your device. Handwriting is read online: a picture of your writing is sent to our server and read there. This is on by default for a signed-in account and you can turn it off at any time in Settings → Handwriting, after which you type your answers; it stays off for an account under 18 until a parent or guardian has confirmed it, and it never runs without an account.

    Nothing is sent until you agree. The first time a picture would be sent — your handwriting, a photo of your working or a photo of a question — the app asks your permission once, naming OpenAI. If you say no, reading is turned off and you type your answers; if you turn it back on, you are asked again before the next picture. Your answer is kept on your device, for that profile, and the app asks again whenever what it tells you changes.

    When you write with a pencil or your finger, what is sent is an image drawn from your own stroke coordinates and nothing else: not the question, not the expected answer, not your name or profile.

    The same setting also covers a photograph. If you attach a photo of work done on paper, or a scanned PDF of it, that photograph is sent to be read by the same service. It is your camera's picture, resized and re-encoded but not otherwise altered — so whatever is in the frame is sent, which can include the printed question, a name written on the page, or anything else on the paper. Keep out of shot whatever you would not want sent, or type your working instead.

    Either way, our server passes it to an outside reading service, OpenAI, through its API. Reading it does not save it: the reading step does not write the picture to our database, our storage or our logs. What the reading step leaves is the text the reading service returned, and any correction you typed, which we keep for 90 days with the record of that answer. (When you then submit the answer to be marked, we keep your original: see the next items.) We ask the reading service not to store it. Under its own terms it does not use it to train its models, but it may hold a copy for a limited time to watch for abuse; we do not control that. Turning the setting off stops the next picture of your handwriting or working from being sent, and it stays off; a picture already sent cannot be recalled. "Practise this" is separate: a photo of a printed question, taken to find practice questions like it, goes to OpenAI the same way after the same one-time permission, but the Handwriting setting does not cover it. It is sent only when you use "Practise this", and that photo is not kept. What the reading service returns is a transcription of your writing, nothing more, and you can correct it before it counts: your mark is always decided by the marking engine on our server, never by the reading service.

  • Reading happens by itself after a pause. Once you have agreed to the reader, are signed in and are online, the page you are writing is read automatically when your pen has rested for about 1.3 seconds, so you do not have to press Read. It is the same reading with the same rules: only the picture is sent, nothing is sent before you have agreed, and nothing is marked until you check the reading and confirm it. To turn it off, go to Settings → Reading my handwriting; you can still press Read my answer yourself. That choice is kept on your device. The reading service never sees the expected answer, and the AI never decides a mark.
  • Your original handwriting and photos, kept with each marked practice answer. When you submit a handwritten or photographed practice answer to be marked, our server keeps your original with the mark, so that there is a record of exactly what was marked. For handwriting that is your pen strokes as your device recorded them (where the pen was, when, how hard it pressed, how it was tilted, and the strokes you erased). For a photo it is the photo or scanned page as you sent it. We also keep a fingerprint of it (a SHA-256 value), which shows it has not been changed; which strokes each line you confirmed came from; and the reading beside it: what Pri read, the text you confirmed and any edits you made.
  • How long an original is kept: 2 years (730 days) after it was kept. Then it is deleted automatically.
  • An original is deleted with your account, and included when you export your data.
  • Who can open an original: only you (it is in your data export). The app has no screen that shows it yet. If you are in a class and hand in work to your class teacher, our server will let that teacher open the original of the answers in the work you hand in, and nothing you have not handed in; the teacher's screen does not show it yet. No other student or teacher can open it. There is no screen in our administrator tools for opening it either, although we, as the operator, can reach the database it is stored in to run the service.
  • A limit on what is kept: one account can have up to 40 MB of handwriting and 24 MB of photos kept. Past that, the answer is still marked, but its original is not kept on our server. Your device still has its own copy.
  • Reading is answer-blind and you confirm it. The reading service sees only the picture, never the expected answer or the solution. You check what was read, and can correct it, before the answer counts. The reading never decides a mark.
  • Help from an AI explainer, when you ask for it. When you open a hint step, a walk-through, "Why this step?", a check of a line of your working or a mistake explanation, or type a question to the tutor, our server sends what is needed to OpenAI: the question, our own worked solution to it (from our question bank, not from you), the lines of working or the answer you have written, and what you typed to the tutor. Not your name, email address or profile. Anything an AI wrote is labelled as written by AI and not checked by the maths engine, and the AI never sets a mark. A copy of a reply may be kept for up to 24 hours so that the same question is not asked twice; a reply to something you typed yourself is kept only for your account. We also record, for 90 days with the question, that you were given help on it, so that your progress can say whether you answered on your own or with help. It is on for a signed-in account whose guardian consent, where one is needed, is in place.
  • Your working and the question, only if you switch that on. There is a second setting, also off unless you turn it on, that sends the lines of working you wrote and the text of the question when an answer is wrong and the app cannot tell you which line went wrong. It is used to tell you where the mistake is. Our server keeps no copy; the checking service handles it as the reading service above does. The expected answer is never sent with it, and your name and profile are never sent with it. Your mark is decided by the marking engine on our server either way.
  • A parent or guardian's name and email address, only for an account of someone under 18, so we can send them the confirmation link or code described under Children and show you, partly hidden, where it went.
  • Device information needed to keep you signed in: a random device identifier this app generates (it is not your device's own id and not anything you typed), a one-way hash of your browser's user-agent string, and the times you signed in and were last seen.
  • Subscription status from the App Store or Google Play, if you subscribe: whether a subscription is active, which plan, and when the paid period ends. Never your card details.
  • Crash reports, while the Crash reports setting is on (it is on for a signed-in account and can be turned off for the device in Settings): the name of the screen that failed, a short error code, a fingerprint of the fault and the app's build number. Never the error message, the page address, the question, your writing or anything you typed.

Children

Pri Learning is used by children. Under the DPDP Act, a child is anyone under 18, and a child's data may only be processed with verifiable consent from a parent or guardian.

What the app does today, plainly: it asks your age but does not keep it, and it never asks for your date of birth. When you create an account it asks your age (11 to 17, or 18 or older) and which class you are in, which tells it what maths to set you. Unless you say you are 18 or older, the account is treated as a child's, and we keep only that decision (child or adult), not your age. It then asks for a parent or guardian's name and email address. We email them a link to confirm the account, or a six-digit code, which they enter on their own page, on their own device, after reading a short notice of what they are agreeing to. Until they confirm, the account can sign in, show and export what it holds, and be deleted, and nothing else: no answer is marked, no handwriting or photo is read, and it does not sync, join a class, send a report, send a crash report, subscribe, or use either server setting. The same email lets them withdraw at any time, which stops all of that at once — marking, reading, exams and sync; a withdrawn confirmation cannot be given again with the same link.

If no parent or guardian has confirmed the account 30 days after it was created, or 30 days after a parent or guardian withdraws, the account is closed: it stops working and every sign-in on it ends. Everything it holds is erased 7 days later. A parent or guardian can also ask us to erase the account without the child's login, from the link in our email or by writing to the address under Your rights; it then stops working at once and is erased 7 days later.

What that confirmation proves, and what it does not. It shows that someone who can read that email inbox followed the link or entered the code. It does not show that they are an adult, or that they are your parent or guardian, so it is not the verifiable parental consent the DPDP Act will require. Our record of it says which method was used, so nobody can mistake it for more than it is.

If you are under 18, please set this up with a parent or guardian and show them this notice, particularly the section on what a cloud account sends. Answers cannot be checked without an account; without one, nothing about your learning leaves the device.

The DPDP Act's obligations on children's data, including verifiable parental consent for anyone under 18, are expected to take effect in May 2027, eighteen months after the DPDP Rules were notified in November 2025. Before then, and before this app is offered to children at scale, the email confirmation has to be replaced by a method that does verify the parent or guardian, and this section rewritten to describe it.

We do not use children's data for advertising, we do not track children across other services, and we do not build behavioural profiles for anything other than choosing the next maths question.

Cookies and storage

We use only what the app needs to work, and nothing to track you.

  • Sign-in cookies. When you sign in, our server sets cookies that keep you signed in on that browser, protect your session against forged requests and, for a moment, show that you have just confirmed your password. They are removed when you sign out. They are not used to follow you around other sites.
  • Storage on your device. The app keeps your profile, your work and your settings in your browser's or phone's own storage so it can open and keep your place. This is what "What stays on your device" describes.

We use no advertising or analytics cookies, no tracking pixels and no third-party trackers.

Who else sees your data

Only the services needed to run the parts you use:

  • Email delivery (Resend) for verification links, password reset links, one-time codes and the confirmation sent to a parent or guardian. The delivery service sees the address and the message, as a postal service sees an envelope.
  • Your class teacher, if you join a class, and only what "Teachers and classes" lists.
  • Apple and Google, if you subscribe through the App Store or Google Play. We never see or store your card details; the store tells us only whether a subscription is active, which plan, and until when.
  • Razorpay, our payment processor for web payments in India. You enter your card, UPI or netbanking details on Razorpay's page, not ours: we never receive or store them. We send Razorpay your account's email address so it can send you its payment receipts, and it tells us whether a subscription is active, which plan, and a reference for each payment.
  • An error-reporting service (Sentry, or a service that accepts reports in the same format), only if our server is set up to send to one. It receives the same allow-listed fields as a crash report (the screen or route that failed, a short error code, a fingerprint of the fault and the build number), never the error message, a question, your writing or anything you typed.
  • Our hosting and database providers (Railway and Neon), which store and process it for us — see "Where your data is stored".
  • A handwriting reader (OpenAI), while the handwriting-reading setting is on and you have agreed to send it, and only the image of your writing or the photo described above.
  • An AI explainer (the same provider), when you ask for help, and only what "Help from an AI explainer" lists.
  • A step checker (the same provider), only while the optional working-check setting is on, and only the working and question described above.

We do not sell your data. We do not share it for advertising.

Teachers and classes

A teacher can see you only if you join their class with its class code. Then, and only while you are a member and your account is allowed to use the cloud:

  • A teacher can see: your name (never your email address); that you are in the class and since when; for each syllabus outcome, where your server-marked work stands; counts of common mistakes across the class, with no names attached; papers you hand in to that teacher (the score, each question's marks, your working as read, line checks and where the first mistake was); the handwriting of the answers in a paper you hand in, where one is kept (our server allows it; the teacher's screen does not show it yet); and the marks and comments the teacher gives.
  • A teacher cannot see: your email address, your guardian's details, your plan, your notes, your conversations with the AI tutor, your practice working, your photos or your practice handwriting, papers you have not handed in, or anything from other classes.
  • You can see the same list. The app shows students what their teacher can see. If you leave the class, are removed from it, or a parent or guardian withdraws consent, the teacher stops seeing you at once.
  • Teacher marks. Where the marking engine cannot award a mark (for example for method), your own teacher may award or decline it. Each decision is recorded with which teacher made it and when, which criterion, how many marks, and the engine's mark at the time. It is kept apart from the engine's mark, added to the record and never written over it, and you can see it. An AI never suggests a teacher mark.

Safety messages

If what you type to the tutor or another help feature suggests you may be unhappy, frightened or in danger, the app does not send it to an AI and does not tutor. It shows a calm message and the support line for your region, Tele-MANAS on 14416, and asks you to tell a trusted adult near you. No one is told that it happened, and the message is not stored. The check is a fixed list of words and phrases, so it can miss things, and it is not a crisis service. If you are in danger now, tell an adult near you or call your local emergency number.

Product measurement and experiments

We count, across all accounts together, how many accounts answered a question, how many came back later, how many free trials became paid subscriptions, and the optional one-tap reasons people give when they cancel. These counts come from records we already hold, are read only by our administrator with a second sign-in check, and are hidden when fewer than 10 accounts are behind a number. The app has no third-party analytics, advertising or tracking code: nothing in it follows you to other sites.

We have built, but not switched on, a test of whether choosing the smallest next hint by a fixed rule helps students more than the usual hint ladder. It is off. If it were ever switched on, each account would be put in one of two groups by a fixed calculation, the group would be noted on your answer records, and your marks and credit would not change.

How long we keep it

  • Data on your device stays until you delete the profile or the app.
  • Learning events on our server are kept while your account is open.
  • The record of each marked answer — the question, your answer and working, the transcription of your handwriting or photo with any correction you typed, and the mark — is kept for 90 days. After that your answer, your working and the transcription are gone; which question it was, whether it was right, the marks and the chapter stay in your learning history while your account is open.
  • Your original handwriting or photo kept with a marked practice answer is deleted automatically 2 years (730 days) after it was kept, or when you delete your account, whichever comes first. The mark and your learning history stay while your account is open.
  • Exam papers, the answers you gave in them and their results are kept for up to five years, or until you delete your account, whichever comes first.
  • Telemetry, including crash reports, is limited to an allow-list of fields and is deleted after 90 days.
  • An account of someone under 18 that no parent or guardian has confirmed 30 days after it was created, or 30 days after a parent or guardian withdrew, is closed then and erased 7 days later, as described under Children.
  • When you delete your account it is deleted at once — there is no waiting period. That removes your profile, your sign-in details and every session, your synced work and answer records, your exam papers, your class memberships and submissions, your subscription link, your telemetry and any guardian's details. Your email address can then be used to create a new, empty account. Deleting the account does not cancel a subscription in the App Store or Google Play; cancel it there, or it will renew.
  • Four things are kept after deletion, with nothing in them that identifies you: the record of any payment you made (amount, currency, date and the store's own reference), which tax and accounting law requires us to keep; any problem report you sent about a question, with everything you typed into it removed; a log entry saying that an account was deleted, with no name or email; and the record of an invitation (an invite code, or an opaque account number), which no longer leads to you once your account is deleted.
  • Backups of our database made before you deleted your account still contain it until they are replaced. Operational logs that contain no personal data may persist.

Your rights

You can ask us to:

  • Show you what we hold about you. The app exports it directly, including your stored original handwriting and photos.
  • Correct anything that is wrong.
  • Delete your account and its data. You can do this inside the app, in Settings → Account, whichever way you sign in: with your password, with your Apple or Google sign-in, or with a code sent to your email address. If you no longer have the app, you can do it at /account/delete-request with a code sent to the account's email address. Either way the deletion is immediate, as described under "How long we keep it".
  • Withdraw consent, after which we stop processing and delete.
  • Nominate someone to exercise these rights if you cannot.

Write to prilearning19@gmail.com. See Grievances for how complaints are handled and how to escalate to the Data Protection Board of India.

Security

Passwords are stored only as bcrypt hashes. Sign-in tokens are stored as one-way hashes. Verification and reset links are single-use, short-lived, and delivered encrypted. One-time codes are never stored — only a keyed one-way hash of each is — and they expire after ten minutes and five attempts. Connections use HTTPS.

No system is perfectly secure. If we discover a breach affecting your data, we will tell you and the Data Protection Board as the DPDP Act requires.
